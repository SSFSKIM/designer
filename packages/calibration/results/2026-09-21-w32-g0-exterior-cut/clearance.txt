W32 G0 — the clearance against the reach, per span
================================================================================================================================================================

Canvas:     320 x 200 CSS px (`scenes.json`, unchanged — W32 X5)
Clearance:  from the rows of results/matrix.json, device px / scale
Reach:      `outerShadowReachPx` at the shipped documents, via `reach.ts` beside this
            file — the runtime's own function, not a reproduction of its arithmetic
σ bias:     the axis's own, at `truncatedSides`: roughly 8 % low for a σ ≈ 17 px shadow read through a 20 px margin

  Every span the bed carries is read through a frame narrower than the reach the runtime
  itself declares. That is not new and it is not a defect of the capture: a shadow whose
  visibility floor is 13 CSS px out at span 32 is invisible long before the frame ends,
  and the σ bias the axis names applies where the SHADOW is still strong at the edge.
  What the admitted-band rule does is stop the TRANSMISSION statistic from summing a
  band the frame only left corners of, which is a separate matter from the σ fit and is
  enforceable per band where the σ bias can only be caveated per cell.

§6. `rrect-ml`'s declaration: UPHELD, narrowed, with its own two predictions measured
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The sentence at issue is *"The shadow axis on this cell is marginal by construction
  and must not be used to fit a shadow constant."* It is upheld, and a
  `$comment-w32-g0` is written BESIDE it in `scenes.json` rather than over it — the
  existing text is committed evidence and this gate corrects nothing in it.

  What the rows CONFIRM. The cell is marginal, and the admitted-band rule says in what:
  the `24-48` band's outer edge is 48 CSS px against 35.5 of vertical clearance and
  47.5 of horizontal, so it is outside the frame in EVERY direction and carries 53 % of
  `T`'s weight. Dropping it moves the span-128 statistic from 0.00487–0.00532 to
  0.00738–0.00889 on the four standard beds — the cell was not reading what the number
  said it was. And span 128 is one component: a constant fitted on this cell ALONE is
  fitted on one geometry at one aspect ratio, which is the declaration's own point.

  What the rows FALSIFY, and it is the mechanism rather than the verdict. The
  declaration predicts *"the truncation guard to withdraw the downward extent here
  (clearance 35.5 px against that reach)"*. It did not: `extentBelowNative` is present
  on 42 of 42 span-128 rows in this generation and `extentBelowWeb` on 39 of 42, and
  the median native downward extent is 27.00 CSS px inside a 35.50 px clearance. The
  shipped reach the runtime declares is 32.89 CSS px on the light document and 34.60 on
  the dark, both INSIDE the clearance — by 2.61 and 0.90 px. The reference's own shadow
  ends about eight px before the frame does, so there was nothing for the guard to
  withdraw. The blurred-edge fit says the same: the native σ residual at span 128 is
  0.0043 against span 96's 0.0069, a fit 38 % TIGHTER than the span the declaration
  offers as the safe one.

  So the declaration is right for a reason it did not give. W32 fits spans 32, 44, 96
  and 128 JOINTLY (charter Design, "Which spans fit and which are read"), which is not
  what the sentence forbids: no shadow constant is fitted on `rrect-ml` alone, the
  `24-48` band is not admitted at that span, and the cell's contribution is one span of
  four in a joint objective. The sentence stands as written and this gate records what
  it now means.

§7. Span 160: what the bed can and cannot see
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The canvas is 320 x 200 and `rrect-lg` is 280 x 160, so every span-160 cell has 19.50
  CSS px of clearance on all four sides against a shipped reach of 42.93 (light) and
  45.69 (dark): the frame ends at 43 % of the way to where the runtime says its own
  shadow does. Two bands are outside it — `12-24` by 4.5 px and `24-48` by 28.5 — and
  the rows show what that leaves: the `24-48` band survives on a median of 3,878 pixels
  at span 160 against 39,518 at span 128, a tenth, and all of them in the capture's
  four corners. Every extent the axis would have reported is withheld on the active
  non-holdout WebGPU population — 0 of 28 on both sides, in all four directions, and
  both offsets with them — so at span 160 the bed can see neither side's reach at all.

  What it CAN see is the transmission at 3–12 CSS px, on a whole annulus, on both
  sides: `3-6` and `6-12` carry 6,374 and 13,194 pixels, more than either carries at
  span 96. That is the reading W32 admits at span 160 and it is a reading of the
  falloff's INNER twelve px, which is about 0.7 σ of a σ ≈ 17 px shadow. What it cannot
  see is the outer falloff, the reach, the displacement, or any σ that is not biased
  toward the window by the axis's own figure — roughly 8 % low. §5.162's span-160 `T`
  and B1's span-160 native σ of 17.317 are both read through that frame; nothing
  recorded is wrong and what was missing is this qualification beside it. The fix in
  kind is a larger canvas, which is a `scenes.json` decision and a native re-capture
  (W32 X5) — Deferred.
