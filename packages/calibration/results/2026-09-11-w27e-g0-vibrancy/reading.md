# W27e G0 — what Apple's `vibrantColorMatrix` actually is, read from the committed dumps (2026-09-11)

The mechanical table is `table.md` (and `table.json` beside it, with per-element geometry and
every flag). It is regenerated by `packages/calibration/scripts/vibrancy.ts`, and
`packages/calibration/test/vibrancy.test.ts` pins how much of the corpus it read. This document
is the reading: what is in the table, what selects between the operators, what the matrices do
algebraically, and what the dumps cannot tell anyone.

The charter (`docs/doperpowers/specs/2026-09-10-w27-coverage-wave.md`, W27e, Decision Log 4 and
12) sends this gate to find "Apple's vibrancy operator … already sitting unread in 58 committed
layer dumps". It is there. It is not on a label.

---

## 1. The corpus, and the first correction

**57 dumps, not 58.** The five committed trees hold 57 layer dumps
(`2026-09-03-w12-lens/layer-dumps` 14, `layer-dumps-ramp` 8 plus its own `scenes.json`,
`layer-dumps-adapt` 13, `2026-09-05-w18-union-contour/probe/layer-dumps` 2,
`2026-09-05-w20-capsule-corner/g0/layer-dumps` 20). The wave's "58" is the count of *files*
matching `vibrantColorMatrix`, which includes `2026-09-03-w12-lens/g1/g1-layer-dump.md`, the W12
report that quotes one. Every one of the 57 dumps carries at least one matrix.

**60 occurrences, 58 of them the same kind.** Three dumps carry two — `photo__glass-over-glass__rest`
has two independent surfaces, and the two tinted capsules each add the tint's own branch — so
57 + 3 = 60.

| what | n | layer | effect | `inputBackdropAware` | `inputClamp` | `inputClampPreserveHue` |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the surface's highlight | 58 | `CASDFLayer` `@2` (or `@3` with a tint) | `CASDFKeyFillHighlightEffect` | `null` | `1` | `null` |
| the author tint | 2 | `CASDFLayer` `@0` inside the tint branch | `CASDFGradientEffect` | `1` | `null` | `null` |

All four input keys are declared on every occurrence (`inputKeysSource: "inputKeys"`), so a `null`
is the filter holding no value, not an absent key. `inputClampPreserveHue` is never set anywhere in
the corpus — the key the charter names is declared 60 times and used 0 times.

**No occurrence is on a content layer, because there are no content layers.** The corpus's entire
class inventory is `NSViewBackingLayer`, `SwiftUI.ImageLayer`, `CALayer`, `SwiftUI.SDFLayer`,
`CABackdropLayer`, `CASDFLayer`, `CASDFElementLayer` and `SDFPortalLayer`. The reference harness
renders no text inside the glass, by an explicit rule in
`apps/reference-apple/Sources/SceneViews.swift`: *"**No text inside the glass.** The components are
bare material bodies. A label would put a glyph rasteriser — two different ones — inside the region
being measured."* That rule is right for a fixture and it is why **the committed dumps cannot
contain a label's vibrancy operator**. What they contain is the vibrancy operator Apple installs on
the *material's own* highlight, plus the tint's. §5 says what that costs W27e and what closes it.

## 2. What the matrices are, algebraically

A `CAColorMatrix` is four rows of five columns, row order:
`out_i = m_i1·R + m_i2·G + m_i3·B + m_i4·A + m_i5`. In all 60 occurrences the alpha row is the
identity and the alpha column is zero, so alpha is untouched and the operator is a map on colour
alone.

**Every matrix in the corpus factors exactly through Rec.709 luma.** Write
`Y(c) = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B`; then every one of the four is

```
out = m·c + g ⊙ Y(c) + b
```

with the largest coefficient the form fails to reproduce being 2.49e-4 (the largest of the four,
on operator 3; the two tints reproduce to 9e-10, which is float32 storage). The fit is the closed
least-squares solution for `(m, g)` with the weights held at Rec.709 and `b` read straight out of
the fifth column, so the residual is a falsifiable statement about the form and not fitted slack.

### 2.1 The two highlight operators

Both are **achromatic**: `g` and `b` are constant across the three channels to 4e-4 and 0
respectively. Writing `a = m + g` (the gain on an achromatic input's level), they are

| operator | n | `m` (chroma gain) | `a` (level gain) | `b` (offset) | black → | mid grey → | white → |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 0, the default | 56 | 1.5000 | 0.1000 | 0.9000 | 0.900 | 0.950 | 1.000 |
| 3, the dark-glass | 2 | 3.0000 | 1.3500 | 0.1500 | 0.150 | 0.825 | 1.500 → clamp 1 |

So, in words, and this is the whole answer to "what does the matrix do":

- **A level map on luminance, affine**: `Y ↦ b + a·Y`. The default operator crushes the entire
  input range into `[0.9, 1.0]` — a 10× compression toward white. The dark-glass operator stretches
  it to `[0.15, 1.5]`, i.e. a 1.35× contrast expansion about black with a 0.15 lift, clipped at 1
  by the operator's own `inputClamp = 1`.
- **A chroma amplification about that level**: `c − Y ↦ m·(c − Y)`, at 1.5× and 3.0× respectively.
  This is a saturation matrix in the ordinary sense — `m·I + (1−m)·1Lᵀ` is exactly the `A` part —
  and it is what "vibrant" names: hue is preserved, chroma is multiplied, and the clamp is what
  keeps a saturated input inside the gamut (pure red under operator 0 lands at R 2.10 before it).
- **Not a desaturation, and not a contrast stretch about mid grey.** Both candidate readings are
  wrong: chroma goes *up*, and the level map's fixed point is white (operator 0: `Y = 1` maps to
  itself) rather than 0.5.

Both operators are the **identity on pure white**: operator 0 exactly, operator 3 after its clamp.
That is a load-bearing observation. If the key/fill highlight emitted pure white with an alpha
ramp, the two operators would be indistinguishable, and they plainly are not chosen at random. So
the effect must emit values below white — the "fill" light at `fillAngle` 3π/4 against the "key" at
−π/4 (both amplitude 0.5, W12's §5) — and the operator decides what those sub-white values become:
near-white on light glass, near-black on dark glass. §5 names the cheap check that settles it.

### 2.2 The two tint operators are not variants of the foreground one

Operators 1 and 2 have `m = 0` to 1e-5: no identity part at all. Their whole output is a per-channel
affine function of the input's luma, `out_i = g_i·Y + b_i` — a rank-one **colorize**. At `Y = 1`
each lands on the seed colour exactly (systemBlue `0.0392 / 0.5176 / 1.000`, systemOrange
`1.000 / 0.5843 / 0`), which is W12 §5's reading confirmed in the unified form. They are also the
only two occurrences in the corpus with `inputBackdropAware = 1`: Apple's author tint is a
recolouring of **the backdrop beneath**, not a layer drawn over it.

This matters for the wave's count. The grounding recorded "four distinct matrices" and read them as
four variants of one operator. They are **two operators of one kind and two of another**. The
distinct-operator count W27e needs is **2**.

## 3. What selects between the two highlight operators

Every axis the corpus varies, against the operator:

| axis | range in the corpus | moves the operator? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| colour scheme | light only (57/57) | **cannot be tested** |
| backing scale | 1x only (57/57) | **cannot be tested** |
| accessibility | standard only (57/57) | **cannot be tested** |
| interaction state | rest ×56, pressed ×1 | no — the pressed dump carries operator 0 |
| author tint | none ×55, orange ×1, blue ×1 | no — both tinted capsules carry operator 0 |
| corner radius | 14, 16, 18, 20, 22 at two sizes (W20's 20 dumps) | no — all operator 0 |
| aspect | 44×44 and 120×44 at the same span | no — all operator 0 |
| span | 32, 44, 48, 56, 64, 72, 80, 88, 96, 112, 128, 130, 160 | **yes, jointly** — not over checkerboard, where the whole ramp is operator 0, but it flips the operator at a fixed dark backdrop |
| backdrop | 7 backgrounds | **yes, jointly** — not on its own either |

**The selector is backdrop tone and span together, and the switch is binary.** Two occurrences out
of 58 carry operator 3: `dark-solid__capsule-button__rest` and `impulse__capsule-button__rest`, both
span 44. Neither axis moves it alone. **Tone alone cannot be the rule**:
`dark-solid__capsule-button__rest` (span 44, tone 0.011711) carries operator 3 while
`dark-solid__rrect-md__rest` (span 96, *the same backdrop at the same tone*) carries operator 0, so
span flips the operator at a fixed backdrop. **Span alone cannot be the rule either**: the whole
48…112 ramp over checkerboard, and every span from 32 to 160 over every backdrop lighter than
mid-dark-solid, stays on operator 0. The darkest solid that does *not* switch is `mid-dark-solid` at
span 44. §3.1 gives the joint law the two axes enter, and it is one already in the repository.

### 3.1 It is not a threshold on backdrop luminance as such — it is the material's own adaptation

The table's tone column is the mean linear luminance of the **committed background fixture over the
declared component region**, read with the harness's own `decodePng` / `componentRegion` /
`interiorLevel`. That is the closest the committed evidence comes to what Apple's `tracksLuma`
actually samples (the region plus `marginWidth`), and it is not the same quantity as either of the
matrix's two backdrop statistics, which the table records beside it:

| scene | span | fixture, declared region | `material.interiorMeanBackdrop` | `shadow.backdropMeanLuminance` | operator |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `impulse__capsule-button__rest` | 44 | 0.003284 | **0.112676** | 0.003788 | **3** |
| `dark-solid__capsule-button__rest` | 44 | 0.011711 | 0.011711 | 0.011711 | **3** |
| `mid-dark-solid__capsule-button__rest` | 44 | 0.059511 | 0.059511 | 0.059511 | 0 |
| `impulse__rrect-md__rest` | 96 | 0.003195 | 0.003195 | 0.003920 | 0 |
| `dark-solid__rrect-md__rest` | 96 | 0.011711 | 0.011711 | 0.011711 | 0 |

**Which reading is used decides whether the selector even looks monotone.** On
`material.interiorMeanBackdrop` the impulse capsule is the *brightest* of the three span-44 cells
and it switches, while mid-dark-solid, darker, does not. On the fixture's own declared-region
reading it is the darkest, and the switch is monotone in tone at fixed span.

The interior axis is the wrong reading on that one cell, for a reason the repository already
documents. `interiorMeanBackdrop` is the backdrop's mean over the **extracted native silhouette**
(`packages/calibration/cli/measure.ts`, `const interior = nativeSil`), and `src/report.ts` says what
that extractor does over a high-contrast backdrop: it "necessarily loses any part of the material
whose level coincides with the backdrop's", punching holes through the interior. Over an impulse
train that leaves almost nothing but the bright pixels themselves — 71 of them, as the cell's own
`interiorStdDevBackdrop` shows, since 0.316196 is exactly `√(p(1−p))` at `p = 8/71`. Measured
against the fixture across the whole corpus, the interior axis is **exact**: it reproduces the
declared-region reading to within 4.6e-5 on 29 of the 30 occurrences that have a matrix cell, and
misses by 0.109392 on exactly one — `impulse__capsule-button__rest`, the cell the selector turns
on.

Both halves are already one law in vitrea. `backdropToneAdaptation(tone, sizeThickness(span))`
(`packages/renderer-webgpu/src/material.ts`) — the texture-collapse strength W9 fitted from pixels,
whose own doc comment says *"the foreground decision has to be taken against the material the
surface actually shows"* — evaluated at the shipped profile on that tone **separates the two
operators completely**:

- on the 2 cells carrying operator 3: **exactly 1.0**;
- on all 56 cells carrying operator 0: **at most 0.007705** (`impulse__rrect-md__rest`; every other
  cell is exactly 0).

The same predicate explains the size dependence without a second rule: the collapse's argument is
`tone + 0.05·sizeThickness(span)`, so a span-96 surface reads its backdrop as 0.05 brighter than it
is, and `dark-solid` at span 96 lands at 0.0617 — above `backdropToneHigh` = 0.055 — while at span
44 it lands at 0.0163, below `backdropToneLow` = 0.02. Apple's own configuration agrees: the two
switching cells are exactly the two in the corpus where the body's `inputFaceColorMatrixFillColor`
flips from **white** to **black** (α 0.2946 and 0.2500), `inputFaceColorMatrixBlack` sits on its
floor of 0.1, and `inputShadowColorMatrixFillColor` becomes `nil`. The vibrancy operator switches
with the glass, not beside it.

**A competing joint law fits the same 58 rows and the corpus cannot separate it.** Apple's own
`tracksLuma` is 1 for every span ≤ 64 in the corpus and 0 above, so the rule "`tracksLuma` = 1 **and**
tone below a threshold in (0.011711, 0.059511]" classifies all 58 occurrences exactly as the
collapse predicate does — the two cells that switch are both `tracksLuma` = 1, and every span-96
cell is `tracksLuma` = 0. The two models differ only in *where between spans 44 and 96* the switch
crosses at a dark backdrop, and the corpus has no cell there. Both are joint laws in tone and span;
neither is a threshold on tone. §3.3 names the ladder that tells them apart.

### 3.2 Is the matrix a function of the cell, or a fixed operator per scheme?

**A function of the cell, through the material's own adapted state — and the dumps see only two of
its values.** It is not a fixed operator per scheme: at one scheme, one scale and one accessibility
mode, two distinct operators appear. It is not a function of geometry as such — radius and aspect
move it not at all, and span moves it only through the collapse argument's `+ 0.05·sizeThickness`
term, never independently of the backdrop. Whether it is a *continuum* sampled at its two ends or a
genuine two-valued switch, the corpus cannot say — there are two positive examples and no
intermediate.

**The threshold G1 declares must be on the joint form, not on tone.** Its location is bracketed
only by the two solids that straddle it, and the bracket is a statement about the collapse
argument: at span 44 (`sizeThickness` 0.09229, so a bias of 0.004614) the switch lies in
`tone + 0.05·sizeThickness(span) ∈ (0.016325, 0.064125]`, which at that one span is
`tone ∈ (0.011711, 0.059511]`. vitrea's fitted `[0.02, 0.055]` smoothstep falls inside it. That is
agreement, not confirmation — and a threshold declared on tone alone would be refuted by
`dark-solid__rrect-md__rest`, which sits at the same tone as a switching cell and does not switch.

### 3.3 What is confounded, and the capture that separates it

- **Scheme, scale and accessibility are fully confounded with everything.** 57 of 57 dumps are
  light, 1x, standard, key window, 8 s settle. Nothing in this corpus can say whether the dark
  scheme carries a third operator, or whether Increased Contrast moves one. `--scenes` plus the
  existing dark and accessibility scene sets makes that a one-line `dump-layers` run.
- **Tone and the collapse predicate are confounded with each other** because only two cells
  switch. A ladder of solid backgrounds between linear 0.004 and 0.060 at span 44 — four rungs
  would do — separates "threshold in tone" from "threshold in the collapse" and, if the operator
  is continuous, shows it. The bed has no such backgrounds today; `scenes-*-probe.json` is the
  pattern for adding them.
- **Span's role is demonstrated but its shape is not.** That span flips the operator at a fixed
  backdrop is measured — `dark-solid` at 44 against `dark-solid` at 96 — but the corpus holds
  exactly that one transition, so the *form* the span enters by (the collapse's
  `+ 0.05·sizeThickness` bias rather than, say, a knee at `tracksLuma`'s own span 56, which the same
  two cells also straddle) is not separated. A span ladder over `dark-solid` — the 48…112 rungs the
  W12 ramp already declares, re-pointed at a dark background — locates the crossing and tells the
  two apart.
- **The label is not confounded — it is absent.** See §5.

## 4. The tier composite question, settled in Chromium

Evidence: `composite-probe/` — `results.json` (every reading, with the resolved `GlassGroupState`
and the adapter report), the page and driver beside it, and one PNG per case. Real Chromium
(`channel: "chromium"`), Apple `metal-3`, `isFallbackAdapter: false`, viewport 1000 × 760 at dpr 1,
on port 5231 (verified free, `strictPort`, worktree-local server — the tracker's shared-port entry).
The scene is built through `platform-web/e2e/fixtures/harness.ts`, so the DOM under test is the
shipped plane sandwich, and every reading is a compositor screenshot, never a canvas readback.

Three configurations, each resolving as asked: `css`/`css-backdrop`, `webgpu`/`css-backdrop`
("gpu-dom") and `webgpu`/`gpu-texture`.

The instrument is a **pair** of blend identities on the same spot, because either alone is
degenerate against a near-white material: white under `multiply` renders `M = (1−α) + α·C`, black
under `plus-lighter` renders `P = α·C`, so `α = 1 + P − M` and `C = P/α`. The pair therefore reports
the blend backdrop's colour *and* alpha in code values. The page ground is a hard 24 px
`#1040c0`/`#f0e000` checkerboard, so the raw page identifies itself by variance (sd ≈ 112) as well
as by mean.

### 4.1 Which buffer — the material's composite, on both tiers

The same pair mounted at each rung of the ancestor chain, one page load each:

| mount | css | gpu-dom | gpu-texture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `document.body` | α 0.996, C = 128,144,96, sd 112 — **the raw page** | same | same |
| the glass root | **α 0** | α 0 | α 0 |
| the plane root | α 0 | α 0 | α 0 |
| the host layer, over the surface | α 0.686, C = 255,255,255 | identical | **α 1.000, C = 197,201,230** |
| inside the host, over the surface | identical | identical | identical |

**On both tiers a blended label composes against the material's composite; the page never enters
the buffer.** The isolation boundary is vitrea's **glass root**, which
`createGlassLayerManager`'s `ROOT_STYLE` makes a stacking context (`position: fixed; z-index: 1000`)
and a stacking context is an isolated group for blending. On the WebGPU tier the group therefore
runs from the glass root down and contains the optics canvas, proved on `gpu-texture`, where the
label reads α 1.000 and C = 197,201,230, byte-equal to the bare material beside it, and a 50 % grey
`multiply` reads 99,101,115 = 0.502 × [197,201,230] exactly. On the CSS tier there is a second,
tighter boundary: `css-tier.ts` writes `isolation: isolate` on the host, so a label there can see
only the host's own children. The `gpu-dom` control isolates that variable directly — adding
`isolation: isolate` to the host drives the label's blend to α 0 while leaving the canvas untouched.

So the grounding's tier note (§Design's advisory: "on the CSS tier the host is `isolation: isolate`
so a blend composites against exactly the material; on the WebGPU tier the host is not a stacking
context and would composite against the root's buffer") is **right about the mechanism on both
tiers and wrong about the consequence on the WebGPU tier**: the root's buffer is not the page, it
is the material, because the glass root isolates.

### 4.2 The catch that dominates the answer

**A `mix-blend-mode` inside the host collapses `backdrop-filter` sampling for a group whose proxy
lives outside that element's subtree.** Measured against the bare-material band, one page load per
declaration. Every case builds exactly one group, so what is *measured* is cross-subtree reach —
the blend sits in the host layer, the proxy it collapses is the sibling `backdrop-proxy` layer
under the same plane root (`planes.ts`). "Every group under the glass root" is the natural
extrapolation from that mechanism, and it is not measured here (§4.5).

| on the label, inside the host | css | gpu-dom | gpu-texture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| none | 232,228,225 sd 3.58 ✓ | 213,222,195 sd 12.59 ✓ | 197,201,230 sd 0 ✓ |
| `filter: url(#vib-default)` | 232,228,225 sd 3.58 ✓ | 213,222,195 sd 12.59 ✓ | 197,201,230 ✓ |
| `background-blend-mode: multiply` | 232,228,225 ✓ | 213,222,195 ✓ | 197,201,230 ✓ |
| `mix-blend-mode: multiply` | **215,220,205 sd 35 ✗** | **215,220,205 sd 35 ✗** | 197,201,230 ✓ |
| filter + blend | 215,220,205 ✗ | 215,220,205 ✗ | 197,201,230 ✓ |

The collapsed picture is a flat white veil at α 0.686 over the raw checkerboard, which the numbers
close on: 0.686 × 255 + 0.314 × 132.5 = 216.6 against a measured 215, and sd = 0.314 × 112 = 35.2
against a measured 35.0. It is causal and reversible — removing the element restores the material
byte-identically on the same page. The mechanism is that blending forces the enclosing group to
isolate, and a render-surface boundary above a `backdrop-filter` re-roots it. A `filter` on the
label is safe because a filter re-roots only its own descendants, and the tier's material layers are
the label's *siblings*. It follows that the α 0.686 white the css and gpu-dom rows report in §4.1 is
the **broken** material, and only `gpu-texture` ever blends against the real one.

Backdrop-root triggers above the label, measured against the same band: `opacity: 0.99` on the host
collapses (confirming contract X6), `filter: blur(0px)` on the host collapses, `mix-blend-mode` on
the host collapses; `isolation: isolate` on the host is **byte-identical to baseline** (the capture
hashes `61578937…`, the baseline's own digest); `contain: paint` on the host does not collapse but
is not byte-identical — it hashes `805be1c5…` and reads 231.87, 227.69, 224.95 sd 3.49/2.50/3.59
against a baseline of 231.88, 227.69, 224.98 sd 3.49/2.50/3.58, a hundredth of a code, which is a
paint-containment rounding difference rather than a sampling one. On the glass root `opacity` and
`filter` collapse. One row is worth keeping: the same
`opacity: 0.99` on `document.body` does **not** collapse (230,226,223 sd 3.58 — just the dimming),
because an ancestor's backdrop root only costs sampling when the content the proxy needs ends up
outside it, and `body` contains the page while the glass root does not. That is a sharper statement
of X6 than "an ancestor opacity kills sampling". (vitrea's own probe disagrees with the pixels on
that one case — it reports `backdrop-root-broken` and demotes; logged to the tracker.)

### 4.3 Can the operator be a CSS `filter` — yes, exactly

`filter: url(#m)` with an `feColorMatrix` and `color-interpolation-filters: sRGB` reproduces the
`CAColorMatrix` **to the code value**, on flat swatches with no glass and no blending. Hand
computation against measurement, both operators:

| operator | colour | `b + a·Y + m·(c−Y)`, ×255 clamped | measured |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| default | `#000000` | 230,230,230 | **230,230,230** |
| default | `#808080` | 242,242,242 | **242,242,242** |
| default | `#ff3b30` | 255,178,162 | **255,178,162** |
| default | `#0a84ff` | 84,255,255 | **84,255,255** |
| dark-glass | `#000000` | 38,38,38 | **38,38,38** |
| dark-glass | `#808080` | 211,211,211 | **211,211,211** |
| dark-glass | `#ff3b30` | 255,50,17 | **255,50,17** |
| dark-glass | `#0a84ff` | 0,245,255 | **0,245,255** |

Every opaque case is a 0.0 difference, including the negative one (`dark-glass` on `#0a84ff` gives
−0.4761 before the clamp). Clamping is plain `[0, 1]` on the encoded channel after the matrix, which
is what `inputClamp` = 1 does. **`color-interpolation-filters: sRGB` is load-bearing**: the SVG
default is `linearRGB`, and the same twenty numbers there are a different operator. Alpha behaves as
non-premultiplied with an identity alpha row, which is what antialiased glyph edges need — 50 %-alpha
black under the default operator over black reads 115 = round(0.9 × 0.5 × 255).

The alternatives, measured rather than assumed: `backdrop-filter: url(#vib-default)` does work in
Chromium (230 over black, 255 over white — the matrix applied to the backdrop), but it is the wrong
semantics and it forms a Backdrop Root, which is the one thing a label must not do;
`mix-blend-mode` cannot express the operator at all, since no blend mode is a per-channel affine
map with luma coupling; `background-blend-mode` is safe, works, and creates no stacking context.

### 4.4 The answer W27e G2 needs

- **The colour transform, both tiers:** `filter: url(#m)` with `feColorMatrix` and
  `color-interpolation-filters: sRGB`. Exact, and safe on every configuration measured.
- **The compositing half, on `css-backdrop` groups** — the CSS tier and the WebGPU tier over a DOM
  proxy, which between them are almost every real surface — **must not be `mix-blend-mode`.** Fold
  the darkening into the ink in closed form on the CPU: both tiers already compute the composite the
  ink sits on, so `multiply(vibrancy(ink), composite)` can be written as a plain `color`, or carried
  by `background-blend-mode` *inside* the label. The composite is not optional — the operator alone
  at source-over renders a 230 grey on a 232 material, which is invisible.
- **On `gpu-texture` groups the literal Apple pipeline works and is exact:** `filter: url(#m)` then
  `mix-blend-mode: multiply` reads 178,181,207 against a material of 197,201,230, and
  0.902 × [197,201,230] = [177.7, 181.3, 207.5].

### 4.5 What the probe does not settle

**The blend's blast radius is measured on one group, not on many.** Every case builds exactly one
group, so "a `mix-blend-mode` inside the glass root collapses sampling for *every* group in it" is
an extrapolation from the mechanism (the blend forces the enclosing group to isolate, and a render
surface above a `backdrop-filter` re-roots it), not a reading. What is measured is that the reach
crosses subtrees — from the host layer to the sibling proxy layer. A two-group page with the blend
in one host would settle it, and it is worth settling before G2 leans on the universal, because the
narrow form alone already forbids the mechanism on a `css-backdrop` group.

Whether a CSS-tier label's blend group would contain L1's *filtered* output if the filter were
alive is unanswerable in principle: any blend inside the host kills L1 and L2, so the two states
never coexist. That the isolation boundary is exactly the glass root is pinned by measurement only
to *at or below* the glass root and *at or above* the host layer; the exact rung rests on reading
`planes.ts`. One engine and one machine (Chromium/Metal, macOS): Gecko and WebKit render
`backdrop-filter` as a no-op in every automatable capture path, so the collapse test has no signal
there. And these are flat slabs, not glyphs — the operator's behaviour on antialiased type is
inferred from the alpha swatches, not measured on rendered text.

## 5. What G0 could not settle, and what G1 must do

### 5.1 The dumps do not contain the label's operator

This is the finding that most changes W27e. The charter's design paragraph says the operator "is
fitted to Apple's `vibrantColorMatrix` as read from the committed layer dumps, not to pixels of
text; the no-text fixture rule stands". The second clause is exactly why the first cannot be done
from this corpus: no dump contains a label, so no dump contains whatever SwiftUI installs on one.

The gap is cheap to close and does **not** require breaking the no-text rule. `dump-layers` reads
configuration and captures no pixels — its own doc comment is *"Nothing is captured, nothing is
written under `fixtures/`"* — so a scene with a `Text` inside `.glassEffect`, dumped and never
captured, puts no glyph rasteriser inside any measured region. What it costs is one optional label
field on the probe spec and the branch in `SceneViews.componentBody` that renders it instead of
`Color.clear`, in a `scenes-w27e-probe.json` of its own; `scenes.json` is never touched, which is
the pattern W9, W12, W18, W19, W20 and W21 each already followed. That run answers three questions
at once: whether a label's layer carries a `vibrantColorMatrix` at all (SwiftUI may instead resolve
a concrete `foregroundStyle` colour); whether it is this family; and whether it is backdrop-aware,
which decides everything in §4.

Until that run exists, the honest scope of a fit from this corpus is **the material's own highlight
tone map**, and W27e G2's "apply the operator to vitrea's own controls' labels" would be applying
an operator fitted to the rim.

### 5.2 The declared partition for G1, and why the dumps cannot supply a real holdout

G0 was chartered to tabulate every occurrence, so every matrix in the corpus is now published: a
holdout drawn from these dumps is spent by construction. It is also nearly powerless — 58
occurrences carry 2 distinct values, so any partition either leaves a 1-cell holdout or removes
the only evidence the second operator exists. The partition below is therefore a **reading
discipline for G1**, and the real unspent check is a native run.

The three parts below partition all 60 occurrences: 55 + 3 + 2.

- **Fit (55 occurrences):** every *highlight* occurrence except the three span-44 solid-backdrop
  cells named below. These fix the operator's form, the default operator's coefficients, and the
  null result on radius, aspect, tint and interaction state.
- **Out of G1's scope (2 occurrences):** the two author-tint colorize matrices. They are a
  different operator family on a different layer, already fitted as the tint pathway in W10 and
  W19, and G1 neither fits nor validates against them. They stay in the table because they are what
  shows the foreground operators are *not* the tint's, and because the tint's is the corpus's only
  backdrop-aware use of the filter.
- **Bracket, not to be consulted until G1's selector is declared and frozen (3 occurrences):**
  `impulse__capsule-button__rest`, `dark-solid__capsule-button__rest`,
  `mid-dark-solid__capsule-button__rest`. G1 declares its selector from the fit set and the
  material's own law, then reads these three once. They are the only three cells in the corpus that
  bracket the switch, and consulting them while choosing the form is fitting to the answer.
- **The unspent checking bed (does not exist; must be captured):** one `dump-layers` run on macOS
  26.5 covering (a) a labelled scene, (b) the dark scheme, (c) 2x, (d) a four-rung solid tone ladder
  between 0.004 and 0.060 at span 44, (e) the W12 span ladder re-pointed at `dark-solid`. Read once
  against a frozen G1 declaration. **macOS 27 ships publicly on 2026-09-14**; per the wave's
  Deferred list the 26.5 bed stays frozen, so this run is either taken on 26.5 before then or keyed
  as `apple-macos-27.0-…` beside it, never in place of it.

### 5.3 What G1 must fit

The operator's *form* needs no fit: it is exact, in Apple's own coefficients, to 2.5e-4. What is
left is

1. **the selector**, declared on a **joint form in tone and span** — a threshold on tone alone is
   already falsified by `dark-solid__rrect-md__rest` (§3). What is open is where the switch lies and
   whether it is a step or the two ends of a continuum. Two candidates fit all 58 occurrences
   equally: `backdropToneAdaptation` at the shipped profile, which separates them with a margin of
   **0.9923** (1.0 against at most 0.007705), and a `tracksLuma`-style span gate crossed with a tone
   threshold. G1 declares which form and what bound before reading the bracket cells.
2. **the endpoints' meaning** — a check that costs nothing: `dark-solid__capsule-button__rest` and
   `dark-solid__rrect-md__rest` are both committed native fixtures over the same background, and
   they differ *in the operator* as well as in the size law. Any rim-tone difference between them
   beyond what the size law predicts is the operator's pixel signature, and its absence would say
   the highlight is white enough for the operator not to act. That settles §2.1's open question
   from evidence already in git.
3. **whether the label carries this operator at all** (§5.1) — and if it does not, what it carries.
4. **X4** — the four published token names keep their names and change only their derivation, so
   G1 states each level as a function of the operator rather than of the scheme.
5. **X9** — how the operator scales with presence. Nothing in the corpus bears on it directly: all
   57 dumps are at full presence, and the one interaction-state dump (`pressed`) carries the same
   operator as its rest twin, so the operator is not a function of interaction state. What the
   corpus does say is that the operator is installed unconditionally on every glass surface and is
   parameterised only by the backdrop adaptation, which means the presence hook X9 requires —
   reaching the app's own colour at presence 0 — has no reference in Apple's configuration and is
   vitrea's own design decision, to be recorded as such under X8.

### 5.4 Smaller things the dumps say that nobody had written down

- The tinted capsules' highlight operator is identical to the untinted one, so **the author tint
  does not enter the foreground operator**. Whatever W27e publishes for a tinted button's label
  cannot cite Apple for it.
- The highlight operator is identical on a merged toolbar group and on a single capsule of the
  same span, and identical on both surfaces of `glass-over-glass` despite their different spans
  (130 and 56) — consistent with §3, since both sit over a photo backdrop far above the switch.
- `inputClamp = 1` on all 58 highlight occurrences is what makes the dark-glass operator's white
  endpoint (1.5) land on white rather than blow out, and it is the only clamp the operator ever
  carries. The `glassBackground` filter's own `inputClamp` (1 or 1.06961) is a different filter's
  input and is not this.
