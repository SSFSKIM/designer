W25 G2 — condition.txt: which rows separate each constant, before any of them is fitted
====================================================================================================

THE RULE. W23's lesson, made a wave rule: the condition of a fit is checked BEFORE the fit, and a
constant no row separates is reported rather than tuned. G0's §6 identification table did this on
paper for a law that did not exist yet; this does it for the three mechanisms as built, with the
levers measured through the code that draws them (`predict.mjs`, which differentiates the renderer's
own functions) and through the renderer itself (the ladder's unit rungs, which render one constant
at one value and read what moved).

Three kinds of lever appear below and they are not interchangeable:

  ANALYTIC   d(quantity)/d(constant) from the material functions. Says what the law would do.
  RENDERED   web(unit rung) − web(baseline rung), the same quantity read off two real captures by
             G0's own instruments. Says what the renderer did. Where the two disagree the rendered
             one is the fit's, because the composite absorbs part of every term.
  READER     whether the instrument can see the quantity on that row at all — G0 §1's validated
             bounds, which are a property of the BACKDROP's step pitch and not of the material.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE SHARE LAW — `sizeScatterHeavyShareThick1x` / `…2x`
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANALYTIC (`share-lever.txt`). The constant enters `kDeep` as `lift · sizeThickness(span)`, so

  span   sizeThickness   kDeep(0)   kDeep(+1)   mix(0)   mix(+1)   dσ/dlift (CSS px)
    32        0.0000      0.4000     0.4000     0.2880   0.2880       0.000
    44        0.0923      0.4050     0.4973     0.3082   0.3167       0.074
    96        1.0000      0.5190     1.0000     0.4878   0.5840       0.842
   128        1.0000      0.6362     1.0000     0.5460   0.6431       0.849
   130        1.0000      0.6440     1.0000     0.5516   0.6480       0.844
   160        1.0000      0.7638     1.0000     0.6497   0.7284       0.689

  Span 32 has lever EXACTLY zero and span 44 has 9.2 % of the constant. That is X5 discharged before
  the ladder ran: no value of this constant can move `rrect-sm` or anything smaller, and the capsule's
  whole exposure is a tenth of it.

WHICH ROWS SEPARATE IT. The quantity is the heavy component's SHARE, and only one instrument reads a
share: reader A, the dot's two-component PSF, which needs an `impulse` backdrop. On the bed as it
stands that is TWO scenes at 1x — `impulse__rrect-md` (span 96, uncollapsed) and
`impulse__capsule-button` (span 44, collapsed, share 0.000 on both sides) — and only the first has a
lever. **The 1x share is identified by ONE row, and it is a validation row.** This is G0 §6's finding
3 arriving in practice: "effectively the uncollapsed heavy share is carried by nothing on the
canonical bed."

RENDERED (`fit-share.txt`). Reader A on `impulse__rrect-md` at 1x, native (2.79 / 19.52 / 0.473):

  lift   sharp   heavy    share
  0.00    1.67    9.08    0.233
  0.24    1.68   11.76    0.325
  0.30    1.66   12.34    0.363
  0.48    1.64   15.22    0.491

  The lever is 0.38 share per unit lift near 0 and 0.71 near 0.48 — the constant is well
  conditioned in the sense that the quantity moves, and badly conditioned in the sense that ONE row
  is what says so.

THE CHECK OFF ITS OWN ROW, and what it costs. Reader C's single-Gaussian width on the two probe
grids' structured backdrops is the only other reading that responds. It runs the OTHER way: the
objective (mean |log σ_web/σ_native| over the thick in-bound rows) is 0.2507 at lift 0, 0.2625 at
0.24, 0.2694 at 0.30 and 0.2769 at 0.48. That is not a contradiction and G0 named the reason
(§2c): a single-Gaussian width read at one pitch is a true reading of a DIFFERENT PART of a
two-component kernel, and vitrea's heavy component is 15.2 device px at the reference's own share
against the reference's 19.5. Raising the share of a too-narrow heavy component adds structure the
reference does not have. The co-constant is `sizeScatterGainMax`, which this wave did not charter —
rung `rG` measures what closing it would buy, and the result is in `g2-findings.md` §2.

  The rows that would condition the share properly are G1's: shape (iii+)/(iv) of the amendment
  takes it from 4 rows at two spans to 44 rows at five spans over both scales (G0 §6).

2. THE LEVEL TERM — `sizeToneLevelFar`
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANALYTIC (`levers.txt`, from the tone response itself). The curve `smoothstep(sizeSpanMax,
sizeScatterSpanMax, span)` is 0.0000 at every span ≤ 96, 0.1040 at 128, 0.1155 at 130 and 0.3520 at
160. So on the canonical 1x bed, minus its holdout, the rows with any lever at all are THREE:
`light-solid__rrect-ml`, `checkerboard__rrect-ml` and `photo__rrect-ml`, all at span 128 and all in
the light scheme's scene list. The two probe grids add 28 more at spans 128 and 160 over thirteen
backdrops each.

RENDERED (`fit-level.txt`, baseline `r0` against unit rung `rC2` at 0.02 encoded). The CONTROL is
the strongest single number in this file: **77 rows at or below span 96 have a rendered lever of
0.000000 codes per unit.** Not small — zero, to the precision the reader prints. The term cannot
reach the bed the rest of the material was fitted on, at any value, and that is what made it safe to
probe against the frozen bed at all.

Above the knee the rendered lever is about 0.98 codes per 0.02 of the constant on `rrect-lg`, which
is 55 % of the analytic move on the response's target: the composite absorbs the rest.

THE CONDITION, and it is the wave's weakest. Fitting `residual = gain · lever` over the 31 rows
above the knee:

  bed           n    least-squares gain   median per-row   RMS before → after
  w9           14        +1.4716              +0.909        1.486 → 0.909 codes
  canon-light   3        +0.6587              +0.113        1.077 → 1.026 codes
  w21          14        +0.0444              +0.069        2.469 → 2.462 codes
  JOINT        31        +0.0994              +0.337        1.965 → 1.945 codes

  (a gain of 1 means the constant is the probe's 0.02.)

  **The light grid identifies it and the dark grid does not.** W9's 14 rows agree on a value and
  fall 39 % in RMS at it; W21's 14 rows fit it to nothing and do not move. That is not two beds
  contradicting each other: G0 §5 measured the dark grid's whole collapsed-to-uncollapsed contrast
  at SIX 8-bit codes where the light grid's is a hundred, and a term worth about two codes at span
  160 is inside that grid's own noise. X7's rule — a thick constant lands on the light patch unless
  the dark bed separates it — is the right reading of this table, and G1's probe set at both scales
  is what would settle it (G0 §6: 118 level rows at 1x, 202–238 with the 2x captures).

THE SHAPE WAS ALSO CONDITIONED, and the wave's chartered shape lost. Decision Log 3 (b) put the term
on the tone response's thin-to-thick BLEND, on the reading that the residual's "sign follows the
backdrop". Rung `rC` measured that form on these same rows: it explains 0.3 % of the above-knee
residual (RMS 2.034 → 2.027) with per-row gains running −1.13 … +0.24 and the two grids disagreeing
in sign. The offset form explains 11 % jointly and 39 % on the grid that identifies it. The reason is
stated in `g2-findings.md` §3 and in the constant's own doc comment: G0's "sign follows the backdrop"
is a reading of the REFERENCE's absolute grading, and the residual against vitrea — whose own deep
value keeps rising to `sizeScatterSpanMax` over the same spans — is backdrop-independent.

3. THE ALONG-SIDE FIELD — `optics.regular.rimAlongSideSlope`
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHICH ROWS SEPARATE IT. The flat solids, and only the flat solids: G0 §4 showed that over
`checkerboard` the same reader correlates 0.85–0.96 with the backdrop's own level under the body,
which is the lens and is a much larger signal. That leaves `light-solid`, `dark-solid` and
`mid-dark-solid` over the thick rrects — 16 thick cells x 4 straight sides = **64 rows**, plus 4 thin
control sides at span 44 and 16 at span 32.

RENDERED (`fit-field.txt`, baseline `r0` against unit rung `rD` at slope 1). Every row asks
`(native − web₀) / (web₁ − web₀)`, so no model of the rim's amplitude enters the fit at all — the
renderer supplies the derivative.

  implied slope over 64 thick sides: median +0.452, mean +0.393, sd 0.235, quartiles +0.247 … +0.597
  lever-weighted mean +0.437

  by side — the antisymmetry check, and the result that validates the FORM:
    top    n=16 median +0.404      bottom n=16 median +0.402
    left   n=16 median +0.470      right  n=16 median +0.469

  Opposite sides agree to 0.002 and 0.001. One field with one constant reproduces four slopes that a
  field linear in position could not, and the 17 % between the top/bottom pair and the left/right
  pair is the box's own aspect (160/96 = 1.67 predicted against 1.97 measured by G0), which is the
  residual the product form leaves and a metric diagonal would have doubled.

  by bed: w9 +0.329, w21 +0.598, canon-dark +0.644, canon-light +0.048 — the last on 12 sides of two
  low-contrast cells, which is the row set G0 §4 already flagged as the one where the term is
  smallest (`light-solid` 1x light range 0.0187 against `mid-dark-solid` 1x dark 0.0345).

THE THIN CONTROLS. 4 sides at span 44 with a worst rendered movement of 0.000029 luma per CSS px at
slope 1, and 16 sides at span 32 whose rendered movement is 0.000000 exactly, which the reader reports as no derivative to divide by. X5 by construction, measured.

4. WHAT NO ROW ON THIS BED SEPARATES, so that G3 does not spend the effort
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- **Every 2x constant.** `sizeScatterHeavyShareThick2x` has no fixture: G0 §6 finding 1 — every 2x
  row that identifies a width is at span 32, 44 or 96, and at dpr 2 `sizeScatterFloor2x` is 1, so
  `kDeep` is already saturated and the lift is clamped away whatever it says. It stays at 0 and waits
  for G1.
- **The sharp σ at 1x.** It is `blurSigma`, a whole-bed constant, and the reference's own sharp
  component is 2.62 device px on the collapsed capsule against 2.79 on the thick rrect — span-flat to
  6 %. There is no thick-only sharp width to fit, and moving the shared one moves the thin capsule,
  which X5 forbids. §2 of `g2-findings.md` states this as the wave's answer to "say why the thin
  capsule cannot move".
- **The knee.** G0 §6 finding 5: its lever is exactly 0 at span 96 and 0.002 above it in every bed
  shape. W25 does not move it and does not need to.
