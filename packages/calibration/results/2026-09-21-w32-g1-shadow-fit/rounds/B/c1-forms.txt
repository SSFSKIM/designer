§5. What G0 recommends, and why — the parent rules (Decision Log 1 (c))
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Form (ii), the per-span bound, at 0.0042.**

  Form (iii) is withdrawn on measurement, as the v1 charter's depth-normalised form
  was. It cannot be stated at span 160 on ANY bed — the admitted bands reach 12 CSS px
  there and the first σ-band ends at 0.7 σ = 12.1 — and span 160 is the span C1 exists
  for: it is where the residual the eye named is largest and where every non-ladder
  cell is holdout. It is also unreadable at span 44 on all four beds and at span 32 on
  two, so the thin regime this wave admits to the fit would carry no shape clause at
  all. And the reason is not the choice of multiples: the clearance in the bed's own
  native σ is 5.5–5.9 at span 96, 2.7 at 128 and 1.1 at 160, so no set of multiples
  buys the same number of falloff lengths at every span on a 320x200 canvas. The form
  is right in principle and the bed cannot carry it; it becomes available when the
  canvas does, which is this wave's first Deferred item.

  Form (i) is one bound across three spans and §5.162 §9's finding N-9 already records
  that it is span-confounded. The admitted-band rule makes it worse rather than better:
  the band SET now differs per span — four bands at 96, three at 128, two at 160 — so
  one number is a promise about three different statistics. It is kept as the reference
  reading and is not the clause.

  Form (ii) changes nothing about what is measured. It is form (i)'s own value read
  against a bound stated per span, which is the minimum change that makes the promise
  true: `no worse at any span than at span 96 today`. It keeps the statistic, the
  exclusions, the population and the order statistic exactly as W31 G1 declared them,
  it needs no re-binning and no assumption about the profile between band edges, and
  it fails today at 128 and 160 on all four beds, by 76–112 % at span 128 and 49–90 %
  at span 160 —
  which is the position B1 was in at its own declaration and is what makes it a clause
  a wave can be judged on rather than a restatement of a residual missed everywhere.

  What adopting it costs, stated plainly: the bound is 0.0042 rather than 0.0045, so
  span 96 passes with 1.7–8.3 % of headroom (2x light 0.00413, 1x light 0.00408, 1x
  dark 0.00399, 2x dark 0.00385) where form (i) leaves 8–14 %. The rule is the
  charter's, so this is what the rule produces on this generation and not a number this
  gate chose; and the thin margin at span 96 is the clause's own statement that span 96
  is not free either — a fit that buys span 128 by widening span 96 fails.
