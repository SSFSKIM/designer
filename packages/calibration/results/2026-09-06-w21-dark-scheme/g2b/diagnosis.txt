W21 G2b — the diagnosis, before the fix. The verdict is (ii): the mapping's conversion is
computed against a level that is not this cell's, and near a black neutral it degenerates.


0. HOW THE NUMBERS WERE GOT
-----------------------------
`diag.mjs` replays `root.ts`'s per-surface chain off the SHIPPED exports of
`packages/platform-web/dist/index.js` — `sizeThickness`, `sizeOcclusionAlphaAt`,
`backdropToneAdaptation`, `toneRespondedSourceOptics`, `adaptedSourceOptics`,
`innerShadowedSourceOptics`, `interiorShadowKeep`, `interiorBandLight`, `cssOpticsFromSource` —
so nothing here is a second opinion about what the tier computes. The backdrop tone sample is
computed from the background fixture PNGs by `backdrops.json`, on `sampleBackdropTone`'s own
definition: the LINEAR mean (`linearLuminance`) and the ENCODED mean decoded once (`luminance`).
The canvas is 320x200 and the backgrounds are drawn at or below 1:1, so the runtime's sample and
this one are the same box filter.

The replay is checked against what actually drew, `g2/canonical-reads/` at 1x (body over the

2. WHY IT IS NOT (i), THE CLAMP'S MISSING DOWNWARD REMAINDER
--------------------------------------------------------------
The clamp binds on exactly one cell of the bed, `checkerboard__rrect-md`, and it costs almost
nothing. The solve's target there is 0.0468 — the native reading to four places — the nominal
composite is 0.0927, the shift drives the neutral to -0.0051 and the clamp makes it black, and the
composite the renderer then draws is 0.0486. The truncated remainder is **0.0018 of linear
luminance**, and the GPU tier's own row is inside every adopted bound with it. A downward alpha
branch mirroring the existing upward one would recover 0.0018 on one cell and would move the GPU
tier, which this gate forbids. It is not the mechanism, and no other dark cell clamps at all: the
seven remaining untinted cells solve to neutrals of 0.0117 … 0.0352, comfortably positive.

`toneRespondedSourceOptics`'s comment about the one-sided remainder is therefore still true as
written. The dark remainder claims §5.88 §3 and §5.90 §3 recorded is real and it is 0.0018.


3. WHY IT IS (ii), AND IN WHICH OF TWO WAYS
---------------------------------------------
The conversion solves for the alpha that reproduces the renderer's linear-light composite once the
page has composited in encoded sRGB, and it solves it at ONE declared level,
`referenceBackdropLuminance` = 0.02. On the dark scheme every neutral the solve produces lands in
0.0000 … 0.0352 — that is, ON the anchor — while the cell's backdrop sits at 0.0037 … 0.5000. The
anchor is inside the tint rather than inside the backdrop, and the conversion degenerates in two
distinct ways, both visible in the `span` column:

  (a) **The span vanishes.** `photo__rrect-md` and `photo__rrect-lg` solve to a neutral of 0.0201,
      whose encoded distance from `E(0.02)` is 0.0005 — under `minimumTintContrast`. The guard
      fires and `cssTintAlpha` returns the source's alpha UNCONVERTED: 0.9053, a linear-light
      lerp's alpha emitted verbatim as an encoded-space `rgba()` alpha. The guard's own doc comment
      says this case is "only reachable from a profile that tints to the reference level", and the
      dark profile is exactly such a profile.

  (b) **The span is solved across the wrong pair of levels.** On `checkerboard__rrect-md` the
      neutral is black, the span is the whole of `-E(0.02)`, and the solved alpha is 0.8388. Over a
      black overlay the two pipelines are `E((1-a)*b)` and `E(b)*(1-a')`, which agree at one `b`
      and nowhere else, and the transfer function's curvature near black makes "nowhere else"
      expensive: at `b` = 0.5 the alpha that would land the renderer's 0.0475 is **0.505**, not
      0.839. The emitted layer is 0.33 of alpha too opaque and the composite lands at 0.0073
      against 0.0486.

Both are one statement: the level the conversion privileges is not this surface's, and on the dark
scheme the distance between the two is the whole of the error. Every failing gate row follows from
it — the four `interiorLevelRatioGpuOverCss` pairs are 0.0486/0.0073, 0.0693/0.0284, 0.0398/0.0269
and 0.0393/0.0269, which are the ratios the gate read (3.90, 2.07, 1.50, 1.49) up to the model's
own idealisation.

There is a second level in the problem that the shipped conversion does not carry, and it is why a
naive re-anchor is not enough. The renderer lerps over the backdrop's LINEAR mean; the `rgba()`
sits over the backdrop-filter's output, which is the backdrop's ENCODED mean. On a solid those are
one number and the distinction is invisible; on the checkerboard they are 0.5000 and 0.5000 in
their own spaces but 0.5000 and 0.2140 in linear light, a factor of 2.3. `BackdropToneSample`
already measures both — `linearLuminance` and `luminance` — for exactly this reason (W9, claims
§5.31), so the conversion can be given the pair rather than one scalar and no new measurement is
needed.


4. WHY IT IS NOT (iii), ONE LEVEL PER SURFACE BEING UNABLE TO CARRY IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------
One level per surface carries it, provided the level is this surface's and both spaces are used.
The `cssA'` / `cssBody'` columns of §1 are the conversion re-anchored at
`{ linearMean: sample.linearLuminance, toneLevel: sample.luminance }`, and the composite lands
within **0.0013** of the renderer's on every one of the nine cells, worst case
`checkerboard__rrect-md` at 0.0473 against 0.0486 (the residual is the neutral's rounding to code 0
and the band's `addedLight`, which the conversion does not carry on either side). The remaining
cross-tier difference is then the per-pixel one the tier has always had — the page composites the
mean of `E` where the renderer encodes the mean — which is a spread, not a level, and is the floor
this module's header has always named.

W16 G1 measured this same re-anchor and rejected it, and that measurement does not govern here.
It was taken on the LIGHT bed under the encoded form, where anchoring on the physical level spent
the tier's single degree of freedom on the mean and cost the slope (`ssimMean` down 0.006 … 0.026).
Since W17 G1 the light bed does not draw the encoded form at all: of 177 committed light captures
exactly ten report `cssTint: "encoded"` — `dark-solid__capsule-button` and `impulse__capsule-button`
and their tinted variants, at both scales — and every one of those is at full backdrop adaptation,
`k` = 1 and `tintAlpha` = 1. At alpha 1 the conversion is anchor-INDEPENDENT by construction
(`cssTintAlpha` returns 1 and `cssTintColor` returns `E(tint)` whatever the anchor is), which §1's
last two columns confirm to the code: 1.0000 and 28, 1.0000 and 12, before and after. So the
re-anchor cannot move a single committed light capture, and contract X3 is safe by arithmetic
before it is safe by measurement.


5. WHAT THE FIX HAS TO BE, AND WHAT GATES IT
----------------------------------------------
Anchor the encoded form's conversion at the surface's own sampled backdrop, as the PAIR of means
the two pipelines actually see, and leave the fitted 0.02 anchor everywhere else.

The gate is `cssTintFormAt(compositeLevel) === "encoded"`, read on the same composite level
`css-tier.ts` reads it on. That predicate is a SUFFICIENT condition for the encoded form — the tier
also falls back to it when there is no interior or no reference filter — so a surface that draws
the linear form is never reached, and the plain-`blur()` engines that keep the encoded overlay at a
high composite level keep the fitted anchor and contract X9 with it. What is left inside the gate
is precisely the population the fitted anchor was never measured on: composites below 0.244 in
linear light, which on the committed bed is the whole dark scheme and ten fully-adapted light cells
that the arithmetic of §4 leaves untouched.
