2. OPERATOR FAMILIES / WHAT DISCRIMINATES THEM
Use the SAME seven measured neutral knots (input 40,56,72,88,104,128,150):
F light A =152,160,168,176,183,195,205; light I=150,157,164,171,178,188,197;
F dark A=69,83,96,108,119,134,146; dark I=60,74,87,100,111,127,140.
F is piecewise-linear encoded interpolation, fixed before chroma fits. These are seven
measured ordinates per endpoint, additional to each family's chromatic parameter count.
I propose THREE uniform families, in order of merit, not three promised solutions:
E3: encoded RGB; L=W.x, v=x-L*1; y=F(L)*1+g(L)*v, then unit-cube clip.
    3 gain ordinates/endpoint, linear between encoded L=63,93,118 codes and held outside.
    Adds scalar-tone-before-chroma decomposition to H3, plus level-dependent radial gain;
    unlike H3, the neutral curve is NOT applied separately to R,G,B.
EH6: same encoded decomposition, g(h) instead of g(L), 6 periodic hue ordinates/endpoint
    at OKLab input hues 0,60,...300. At neutral the chroma vector is zero.
    Direct competing explanation: hue-dependent rather than level-dependent chroma gain.
O12: H3prime's OKLab L tone, with its 2x2 (a,b) matrix interpolated across THREE input L
    nodes cbrt(.05,.11,.18): 12 coefficients/endpoint, rather than H3prime's 4.
    Tests level-dependent anisotropy/rotation in that working space, not just scalar gain.

Conclusion: a spatial body/edge conditioner must retain local backdrop AND directional/
position dependence or boundary-tail uncertainty. Subtracting one deep RGB triple would
misassign several body codes to the edge. Conversely, this is NOT proof of a group term:
both gradients have the same mean/distribution, so group-mean dependence is not varied.
Their common-mean mirror difference cannot be fixed by a term depending only on that mean.
No-glass is the external unblurred reference, not a measurement of Apple's internal blurred
sample. Kernel, lens, boundary tails and material response are not separately identified.
The shipped group solve is inadequate as a complete explanation; replacing its argument
with per-pixel luminance is ALSO not established by this experiment.

5. WHAT THIS ARCHIVE DOES NOT IDENTIFY
- Hue interpolation between the six factorial directions; matched-channel cells add some
  directions, not a dense hue sweep. No arbitrary hue harmonics are licensed by six anchors.
- A C response above.024: two remote red/green bridges do not form a chroma sweep or fix
  high-C behaviour in other hues. C12/C24 quantisation alone permits degree-scale angles.
- Full neutral behaviour below40/above150, nor the black branch below encoded.003.
  Scalar bridge lumas within the interval do not identify those extrapolations.
- An arbitrary group-level term: one gradient amplitude/mean, no textured, frequency,
  nonlinear-gradient or chromatic-gradient controls in this read. Blur/response factorisation
  and a group-mean coefficient remain unresolved; two-stage tone is similarly nonunique.
- General span/shape, other accessibility modes, dynamics or stacking. This memo reads
  only the span44 colour/gradient cells; validation span96 and all holdout remain unopened.
- Separation of directional deep-body variation from long boundary influence: even the
  >=14 strip shows active directional differences. No 'edge-free' baseline is certified.

CSS estimates are algebraic projections, NOT browser measurements or shipped CSS scores:
E3/EH6 on a known uniform backdrop can choose saturate s and neutral rgba(alpha,n) so
(1-alpha)*s=g and (1-alpha)*L+alpha*n=F(L), PROVIDED the intermediate saturate does not clip.

maxima remain .618/.832/1.010/1.426. But LIGHT green does clip before the plate: choosing
minimal white-plate alpha gives CSS(133.72,245.89,133.72) A against native(96,246,96),
and CSS(115.31,238.30,115.31) I against(89,239,89):37.718/26.314-code weak-channel misses.
That is a specified direct projection, NOT a lower bound over every possible filter chain.
Do not reintroduce W31's declined saturation mirror while ignoring this clipping.
EH6 additionally cannot vary its hue gain pixelwise across a multicolour backdrop using
one uniform filter/plate. Even its uniform native trial retains .615/.833/2.394/1.677.
O12 cannot be exactly expressed by saturate plus one plate: an arbitrary L-dependent 2x2
OKLab map is not one encoded RGB saturation matrix. Its trial already misses26.217/29.654
on dark bridges before CSS approximation; a native CSS residual is UNMEASURED, not invented.

scales still has minimax residual2.333 light A / .625 light I /2.214 dark A / .773 dark I.

blur/lens/boundary operators. Active directional residual cannot be hidden in 'CSS equivalent'.

a nonuniform backdrop. Any derivative-at-group projection must remain an explicit residual.
