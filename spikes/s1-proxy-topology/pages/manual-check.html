vitrea S1 — manual conformance check

Open this file directly in the browser under test — real Safari 26, real Firefox — not inside an iframe and not through a preview pane: an enclosing iframe or a host page's own compositing can change the very thing being measured. Then answer the amber questions in order.

Why by hand: every automated capture path tried on this machine (Playwright Firefox and WebKit headless and headed, retail Firefox --screenshot, retail Firefox over WebDriver BiDi, and the system WKWebView's takeSnapshot) renders backdrop-filter as a complete no-op, while filter, mix-blend-mode and opacity all render correctly in the same images. So your eyes are the only instrument left for these two engines.

If nothing on this page looks blurred at all, stop and report just that — it is the single most important signal, and it means the engine (or this machine's configuration of it) is not delivering the effect.

A · Sampling equivalence — portaled masked proxy vs in-place filter

Left: backdrop-filter on the host element itself. Right: the host is transparent and the filter lives on one portaled, clip-path-masked proxy painted below a transparent canvas and the semantic host DOM. Same scene, same shape, same filter.

A1 — Do the two panels look the same? Look especially at the outer ~20px ring of each panel, and at the top and bottom edges where the checkerboard and the colour bars meet the glass.

A2 — Padding sweep. All tiles have the identical panel shape and the identical filter; only the proxy's box grows outward behind the mask. Reading left to right, from which tile onward do the panels stop changing?

B · Double filtering — does a later proxy sample an earlier proxy's output?

Flat #3a5a80 ground, filter brightness(1.25), no blur, so the answer is exact arithmetic rather than a judgement. Left: one proxy over the whole strip. Right: two proxies whose masked regions overlap in the middle third.

B1 — In the right tile, does the middle overlap band match swatch ONE or swatch TWO?

B2 — The contract case: two glass panels 8px apart, each proxy's box padded 60px so it covers a slab of the other panel's masked region. Do the two panels on the right look identical to the single-group pair on the left, or is either panel's gap-facing edge brighter or more saturated?

C · Mask extent — does the padding show as a halo?

C1 — The right tile masks its proxy to the padded box instead of to the panel shape. Does a blurred rectangle stand proud of the rounded panel?

D · Backdrop-root breakers — which ancestor styles kill the proxy?

Each tile puts one style on the GlassRoot element — an ancestor of the proxy but not of the scene content. If the engine treats that style as backdrop-root-forming, the tile's panel loses its blur entirely. GlassRoot is given full tile geometry so a clip-path or contain cannot simply clip the plane away and masquerade as a re-rooted backdrop. The first tile is the control and must be blurred.

D1 — List the labels of every tile whose panel is NOT blurred.

E · Overlay plane over base plane

E1 — In the right tile a wide overlay-plane panel crosses the base panel, painted above the base plane's proxy and its tint. Where they overlap, is the result visibly blurred twice — mushier or paler than the base panel is on its own in the left tile?

F · Robustness — scroll, fixed content, a scroller, and zoom

A sticky glass panel over 1400px of scrolling content, including a position: fixed stripe and an inner overflow: auto scroller.

F1 — Scroll the box slowly. Does the blur keep tracking the content underneath, with no stale, frozen, smeared or one-frame-late region?
F2 — Scroll the inner orange/blue striped box. Does the blur track that too?
F3 — Zoom the browser to 150% and 200% (Cmd +). Does the panel stay blurred, and does the blur still look right — no lost filter, no wrong-scale blur?

G · Canvas above the proxy

A layer painting rgba(255,255,255,0.30) sits above the proxy. If the proxy ever sampled it, the result would be brighter than plain source-over compositing.

G1 — Does the right tile's band match the "expected" swatch, or is it brighter?

H · The two-layer body — sibling opacity, mask-image and plus-lighter on a filtered layer

vitrea's CSS tier is moving from one backdrop-filter on the host to two sibling filtered layers — a sharp one and a heavy one drawn after it, so that the second filters the first's output and the pair composes the reference's two-component body (W16). The second layer's weight has to be carried by something, and the lift wants an additive ring. These four questions ask which of those an engine actually performs; Chromium's own answers are recorded in W16 G0 and are noted under each question so a divergence is visible.

Flat #3a5a80 ground and brightness(1.25) everywhere, no blur — so each answer is exact arithmetic against a swatch and not a judgement. Both layers are brightness(1.25); the second filters the first's output, so the heavy component is 1.25 x 1.25 of the ground.

H1 — Sibling opacity. The second layer's weight is opacity: 0.4. Does the band match swatch BOTH, SHARP ONLY or HEAVY FULL? (Chromium: BOTH.)

H2 — mask-image on the filtered layer. The same weight, carried by a uniform mask-image of alpha 0.4 instead of opacity. Same three swatches: BOTH means the mask and the filter compose; SHARP ONLY means the mask killed the filter; HEAVY FULL means the mask was ignored. (Chromium: BOTH.)

H3 — mix-blend-mode: plus-lighter on a filtered layer. One layer, backdrop-filter: brightness(1.25), opacity: 0.4, mix-blend-mode: plus-lighter. Does the band match NOT ADDED (the ordinary source-over result) or ADDED? (Chromium: NOT ADDED — the blend mode blends the element's own content, which an empty layer has none of, so it never reaches the filter's output.)

H4 — a blended parent around the filtered layer. The filter is on a child; mix-blend-mode: plus-lighter is on its parent. Does the band match INERT (exactly the bare ground, i.e. the parent re-rooted the backdrop and the child's filter saw nothing) or ADDED? (Chromium: INERT.)
