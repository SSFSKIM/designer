# Dieter Rams: ten principles for good design (with Rams' own commentary)

Retrieved from Vitsœ's own page, "Dieter Rams: ten principles for good design" — the
version that carries Rams' elaborations, not the bare poster list.

## Context Rams gives for why the principles exist (paraphrase + partial quote)

Paraphrase: In the late 1970s Rams grew concerned about what the page calls (quoting
Rams) "an impenetrable confusion of forms, colours and noises" in consumer goods. This
led him to ask himself a direct question, quoted verbatim:

> "is my design good design?"

His answer, built out over time, became the ten principles below. This is useful as
grounding evidence that the principles are self-imposed operational criteria — Rams
describes them as a personal test he applied to his own work, not a marketing slogan
invented after the fact.

## The ten principles, each with Rams' commentary (direct quotes)

### 1. Good design is innovative
> "The possibilities for innovation are not, by any means, exhausted. Technological
> development is always offering new opportunities for innovative design. But
> innovative design always develops in tandem with innovative technology, and can
> never be an end in itself."

Operational reading: innovation is instrumental, gated by real technological change —
not a standalone design goal. This rules out novelty-for-its-own-sake as a design law.

### 2. Good design makes a product useful
> "A product is bought to be used. It has to satisfy certain criteria, not only
> functional, but also psychological and aesthetic. Good design emphasizes the
> usefulness of a product whilst disregarding anything that could possibly detract
> from it."

Operational reading: usefulness is explicitly multi-dimensional (functional +
psychological + aesthetic), and the design action described is subtractive —
"disregarding anything that could possibly detract."

### 3. Good design is aesthetic
> "The aesthetic quality of a product is integral to its usefulness because products
> we use every day affect our person and our well-being. But only well-executed
> objects can be beautiful."

Operational reading: aesthetics is not decorative add-on but tied to execution
quality — "well-executed" is the precondition for "beautiful."

### 4. Good design makes a product understandable
> "It clarifies the product's structure. Better still, it can make the product talk.
> At best, it is self-explanatory."

Operational reading: legibility of structure is the design's job; the best-case
target is a product that requires no instructions.

### 5. Good design is unobtrusive
> "Products fulfilling a purpose are like tools. They are neither decorative objects
> nor works of art. Their design should therefore be both neutral and restrained, to
> leave room for the user's self-expression."

Operational reading: explicit rejection of the product as artwork or decoration; the
restraint is in service of the *user's* expression, not the designer's.

### 6. Good design is honest
> "It does not make a product more innovative, powerful or valuable than it really
> is. It does not attempt to manipulate the consumer with promises that cannot be
> kept."

Operational reading: honesty is defined against a specific failure mode — exaggerated
claims via form (implying more innovation/power/value than is real).

### 7. Good design is long-lasting
> "It avoids being fashionable and therefore never appears antiquated. Unlike
> fashionable design, it lasts many years – even in today's throwaway society."

Operational reading: fashion-avoidance is the mechanism, not a byproduct — longevity
is achieved by deliberately not chasing trend cycles.

### 8. Good design is thorough down to the last detail
> "Nothing must be arbitrary or left to chance. Care and accuracy in the design
> process show respect towards the user."

Operational reading: detail-level rigor is framed as an ethical stance toward the
user ("respect"), not craftsmanship for its own sake.

### 9. Good design is environmentally-friendly
> "Design makes an important contribution to the preservation of the environment. It
> conserves resources and minimizes physical and visual pollution throughout the
> lifecycle of the product."

Operational reading: environmental responsibility is scoped across the full product
lifecycle, and explicitly includes "visual pollution" as a category alongside
physical/material pollution.

### 10. Good design is as little design as possible
> "Less, but better – because it concentrates on the essential aspects, and the
> products are not burdened with non-essentials. Back to purity, back to
> simplicity."

Operational reading: this is the summary law the other nine feed into. "Less, but
better" is presented as a consequence of essential-aspects focus, not an aesthetic
preference — the test is whether an element is essential, and non-essentials are
treated as a burden to be removed.
