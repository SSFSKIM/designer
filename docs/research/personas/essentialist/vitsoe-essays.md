# Vitsœ's essays on design ethos: longevity, honest selling, the 606's rules

## On the company's core mission statement

Direct quote (vitsoe.com/us/about/ethos):

> "living better, with less, that lasts longer."

Direct quote — what the company positions itself against:

> "a world that has favoured new over better"

(quoted as retrieved; source paraphrased this as Vitsœ "standing up to" that world).

Paraphrase: the ethos page frames Vitsœ's furniture as "discreet, adaptable and
faithful servants" designed to minimize environmental impact specifically through
longevity — i.e., environmental responsibility is routed through product lifespan,
not through material sourcing alone.

## On sustainability as an adopted external standard, not a marketing word

Paraphrase: the ethos page explicitly adopts the Brundtland Commission's 1987
definition of sustainable development, and contrasts a "long-term outlook" against
"built-in obsolescence" as the two poles of the argument.

Paraphrase: products use recyclable materials assembled with mechanical joints
(screws/pins) rather than permanent bonding (adhesives), specifically so that
products can be repaired and disassembled rather than discarded — reuse is called
the company's "touchstone," ranked above recycling.

## On never selling aggressively — the honest-pricing essay

Direct quote (vitsoe.com/us/honest-pricing):

> "we do not price our products in such a way that we can offer 'discounts' against
> initially inflated prices."

Direct quote:

> "our stock does not become obsolete, we never have a 'sale'."

Paraphrase: the essay argues competitors rely on inflated "trade price lists" to
justify discounts later, whereas Vitsœ's position is that it has, in its own words
(quoted): "already discounted our prices for everyone" — i.e., one fair price for
every customer, no negotiation, no seasonal markdown cycle.

Direct quote — on declining to offer payment plans:

> "it would not be honest to charge all customers for a service that is not used by
> everyone."

Paraphrase (from the ethos page, same theme): Vitsœ explicitly does not pay sales
commission, on the stated reasoning that commission "rewards short-term thinking" —
this is the concrete mechanism behind "never sell aggressively": the incentive
structure for the salesperson is deliberately built to remove any reward for
pushing a bigger, faster sale.

Direct quote — founder-level framing of the same idea (from about/general page,
paraphrase-attributed to the founder's stated observation):

> "Our happiest customers are those who have dealt with Vitsœ the longest."

## On restraint in customer communication

Direct quote (vitsoe.com/us/about page):

> "You will only hear from us when we have something of interest to share."

Paraphrase: this is presented as a deliberate rejection of frequent marketing
cadence — restraint applied to communication frequency, not just to product form.

## On the 606 system specifically as embodied philosophy (see also
rams-braun-cases.md for the technical/standardization detail)

Direct quote — the stated lifecycle principle for the product:

> "Start small. Add to it. Take it with you. Reconfigure it. Hand it on."

Paraphrase: Vitsœ frames "buying less initially" as compatible with its own
commercial interest, not opposed to it — the essay's argument is that encouraging a
smaller first purchase, backed by the promise of future reconfiguration support,
builds the long-term relationship that is the company's actual measure of success,
rather than maximizing the size of any single transaction.

## "60 years of principled practice" essay — on what the object itself must do

Direct quote — the founding difficulty:

> "It's been a tough journey, all the way…"

> "It has never been easy trying to do something that has not been done before."

Direct quote — Vitsœ's own definition of its identity, explicitly rejecting a
sales-volume framing:

> "Vitsœ has never been defined solely as a furniture manufacturer. Nor has it been
> interested in a business model focussed on the increase of sales purely for
> financial benefit."

Direct quote — the design target stated in the essay:

> the object must achieve "clarity, order and truthfulness" without "disguise and
> confusion," while ensuring "ease of use, while barely demanding attention."

Paraphrase, attributed to filmmaker Gary Hustwit (quoted within the essay, on the
purpose of the minimalism): the point of the restraint is to let objects "blend
into the scenery," staying "neutral and unobtrusive" — the essay frames this as
prioritizing "nature above all this other stuff" over the object asserting itself.

Paraphrase: the essay explicitly connects Vitsœ's position to Viktor Papanek's
critique of design that exists merely to stimulate consumption, and cites Rams'
"less but better" as the counter-principle.

Paraphrase, attributed to Mark Adams (quoted within the essay): Adams describes
resisting pressure from "instant-gratification customers" who want a fast purchase
without deliberation, treating that kind of transaction as contrary to the
company's actual design ethics — i.e., a considered, slower purchase decision is
treated as evidence the system is working as intended, not friction to be removed.
