# Interviews: Rams on process and decision-making, and Ive on Rams

Brief called for prioritizing the Rams–Ive conversations. The primary Vitsœ-filmed
Rams–Ive video conversation itself was not locatable as a transcript in this session
(search turned up references to its existence — e.g. via the "Less and More"
exhibition materials — but no verbatim transcript text). What follows instead
combines: (a) Jonathan Ive's own written/spoken commentary on Rams' process — most
substantially his foreword to the book "Dieter Rams: As Little Design As Possible,"
archived at archive.org — and (b) Rams' own quotes on process and decision-making
from other interviews. Biography is deliberately excluded except where load-bearing
for a quote's context.

## 1. Jonathan Ive on Rams' process (source: archive.org, "Jonathan Ive on Dieter
Rams" — full text of Ive's spoken/written commentary on Rams, likely drawn from the
book foreword and companion talk)

On first encountering Rams' design as a child (describing the Braun MPZ 2 Citromatic
juicer):

> "It was white. It felt cold and heavy. The surfaces were without apology, bold,
> pure, perfectly-proportioned, coherent and effortless."

> "At a glance, you knew exactly what it was and exactly how to use it."

On the scale of Rams' output at Braun — this is a process/organizational point, not
biography:

> "Dieter Rams and his team at Braun did was to produce hundreds of wonderfully
> conceived and designed objects: products that were beautifully made in high
> volumes and that were broadly accessible."

On simplicity as a design *result*, not an *absence*:

> "Simplicity, of course, is not the absence of complexity. Just removing clutter
> would result in uncomplicated but meaningless products."

On the difficulty of consistent output at volume:

> "To produce more than 500 [significant objects] borders on the absurd."

> "He remains utterly alone in producing a body of work so consistently beautiful,
> so right and so accessible."

## 2. Jonathan Ive interview excerpts (source: rams-foundation.org, "Jonathan Ive" —
official Rams Foundation interview page)

On what Rams contributed beyond individual products — an explicit process claim:

> "One of the things that Dieter Rams, I think, doesn't get credit for, is the way
> that he innovated with process."

> "Dieter Rams in so many ways articulated the framework for the profession that was
> inspirational and responsible."

On team-building as part of the design method (paraphrase): Ive credits Rams'
achievement in building design teams and defining professional practice within
Braun as directly shaping the consistency of the output — i.e., the process
(assembling and running a design department) is treated as inseparable from the
resulting objects.

On refusing to single out one favorite object, valuing the whole body of work:

> "To make one thing well is an achievement. To design 400 is extraordinary...his
> body of work speaks to such a clear sense of how it should be."

On the designer/leadership relationship as a structural condition for good design
(paraphrase, with one direct clause): Ive compares his own working relationship with
Steve Jobs to Rams' relationship with Erwin Braun, framing a close designer–CEO
relationship as necessary to ship "holistic, integrated products rather than
compromised assemblies" — i.e., decision-making authority concentrated with the
designer, not distributed through committee, is presented as a precondition for the
work.

## 3. Dieter Rams, in his own words, on process and decision-making

### On preparation preceding production (source: Fast Company interview, retrieved
via WebSearch excerpt — original article page returned HTTP 403 on direct WebFetch)

> "In industrial design, everything for the production has to be clarified in
> advance with models and prototypes, all the details, for multiple parts."

> "Think carefully in advance about what you're making and how you will make it,
> because for both architecture and industrial design, the cost of changing things
> afterward is much higher than the cost of better preparation."

### On detail as the actual substance of the work, not a finishing touch

> "My heart belongs to the details. I actually always found them to be more
> important than the big picture. Nothing works without details. They are
> everything, the baseline of quality."

### On simplicity as an ongoing discipline (source: Dezeen headline interview quote
and related aggregation, retrieved via WebSearch excerpt — Dezeen page itself
returned HTTP 403 on direct WebFetch, so these are the exact fragments the search
tool surfaced, not a full transcript)

> "Simplicity is the key to excellence!"

> "design that is as reduced, clear and user-oriented as possible"

> "less, but better things"

Paraphrase note: the same aggregation attributes to Rams a line about market
excess — quoted verbatim as surfaced: "ruthless exploitation of people's weakness
for visual and haptic signals" — used by Rams to characterize the design he was
reacting against, not his own practice. Flagged here explicitly so it is not later
misattributed as a Rams design law rather than a critique of others.

### On living with your own designs as a feedback method

> "When you live with products, you get to learn their faults so you can improve
> them."
