# svgf-design Skill — Design Spec

## Purpose

Ship `svgf-design`: a Claude Code Agent Skill that turns SVG filters into a designed material system — Apple-Liquid-Glass-class engineered refraction, gooey/organic forms, grain/print texture, and specular lighting — for the design work AI systems reliably avoid. This is deliverable #2 of the SVGF-Design repo, built on top of deliverable #1 (`figma-design`, shipped and verified 2026-07-25 by beating real Figma Make 2–1) together with the research corpus in `docs/research/svg-filter-atlas.md`.

The skill is a **material specialist that composes with figma-design**: invoking it guarantees at least one signature filter-material moment in the output. The invocation itself is the "earning" that `references/effects-policy.md` (deliberately conservative, and explicitly written to be extended by this skill) demands — the effects policy governs *placement and dosage*, never *whether*.

## Locked design decisions (from the 2026-07-25 grill)

1. **Identity** — material specialist composing with figma-design; a signature filter-material moment is mandatory on every invocation; hard runtime dependency on figma-design (fail loudly if absent, never degrade silently).
2. **Home register** — aesthetically neutral briefs default to refined-tech: restrained, physically plausible optics (the Apple-neat family). Other material families unlock only via stance or brief.
3. **Realism** — physical plausibility is the standard: engineered refraction (computed displacement maps, not turbulence wobble), correct specular edges, plausible frost. Boldness spent in one place: one unmistakable signature surface, everything else quiet. Stylized/non-physical looks unlock only when the stance demands them.
4. **Surface policy** — engineered glass may sit on floating interactive chrome (toolbars, tab bars, sheets, palettes — Apple's actual use), under a hard legibility + fallback contract. Decorative wobble/goo on controls and any displacement of body text stay banned.
5. **Motion** — refraction reads as alive via content scrolling beneath it (zero extra cost). Filter parameters may transition on interaction (hover/press morphs, one goo delight moment) via class toggles between precomputed states. No perpetual ambient loops. `prefers-reduced-motion` fallbacks mandatory.
6. **Browser posture** — tiered glass: Chromium ships full engineered refraction (`backdrop-filter: url()` is Chromium-only, verified 2026-07 in the atlas); Safari/Firefox ship premium frosted glass (blur+saturate `backdrop-filter`) with the same geometry, specular edge, and tokens. Both tiers must look designed, not degraded. **Gating:** the frost tier is `@supports (backdrop-filter: blur(1px))`-gated (atlas §4.3); the refraction tier must NOT be `@supports`-gated — `@supports (backdrop-filter: url(#x))` validates syntax, not rendering, and Firefox parses it while rendering nothing (atlas §2.8 caveat). The refraction tier is capability-gated: either a layered no-op overlay (refraction on a separate element whose `backdrop-filter: url()` harmlessly no-ops in Safari/Firefox) or a tiny one-time runtime probe — the mechanism is chosen and verified in all three engines during the spike.
7. **v1 material families** — glass/refraction (core) + goo/metaball/organic + grain/paper-print (incl. duotone, halftone, ink bleed) + specular/diffuse lighting (mostly serving glass edges; standalone only for badges/seals/material studies). Squigglevision and standalone color-grading → v2.
8. **North stars** — Apple Liquid Glass (visionOS/iOS-26 material system), kube.io-class engineered web glass, Awwwards-experimental expressive (the goo family's ceiling). The dark-SaaS restraint register (Linear/Vercel) deliberately not a north star.
9. **Glass ban line** — plain frost is legitimate only as (a) the Safari/Firefox fallback tier and (b) quiet supporting surfaces. Banned outright: glass over blank/gray grounds, glass card grids, the translucent-white-fill + 1px-white-border 2021-glassmorphism combo as a default card style.
10. **Grounds** — glass requires a visually rich ground; the skill teaches all three sources: authored SVG/gradient art (guaranteed self-contained baseline), real photography when the environment allows, simulated product content (map, video frame, canvas, chart field — truest to Apple's model).
11. **Eval** — A/B on the same briefs: figma-design alone vs figma-design + svgf-design, same worker model both arms, human-judged side-by-side grid. Success: the svgf arm visibly adds the material dimension without losing the base skill's neatness.

Derived (design-time, user-vetoed nothing): register ceilings — product UI caps at Apple-neat, campaign/brand surfaces may reach the Awwwards ceiling. Note: figma-design has no named brand-vs-product register mechanism (its classifier routes by deliverable type; product-type earning logic lives diffusely in effects-policy.md), so `materials-map.md` must **define** the product-UI vs campaign/brand classifier itself, not reference one. Also derived: a stance→material mapping covering all 18 figma-design stances; dosage budget of 1 signature + ≤2 supporting moments; QA material rules split honestly into grep-checkable and visual-browser-QA halves (see acceptance #5).

## What "done" looks like (acceptance, phrased as behavior)

1. **Triggering.** A session prompted "make a landing page with liquid glass navigation" or "give this a gooey, organic feel" loads svgf-design; a plain "make a landing page" with no material language does not (that's figma-design's territory).
2. **Composition.** Every svgf-design transcript shows the figma-design workflow ran first (sampler, stance commitment, DESIGN.md) and that DESIGN.md contains a material-commitment block immediately after the stance commitment: family, signature surface, dosage budget, ground plan, fallback-tier plan, register ceiling.
3. **Signature guarantee.** Every output contains at least one filter-material moment implemented with actual SVG filter primitives (not CSS `blur()` alone) — verifiable by grepping the artifact for `<filter` / `filter: url(` / `backdrop-filter: url(`.
4. **Engineered glass.** When the family is glass, the displacement map comes from `scripts/make-glass-map.mjs` (self-contained data-URI `feImage` → `feDisplacementMap`, optically flat center) — not hand-tuned `feTurbulence`. The page carries both tiers sharing geometry and tokens: an `@supports (backdrop-filter: blur(1px))`-gated frost tier, and a refraction tier capability-gated per atlas §2.8/§4.3 (no-op overlay or runtime probe — never `@supports (backdrop-filter: url())`, which false-positives in Firefox).
5. **Bans hold.** The built page passes the material QA against its own DESIGN.md law, in two honest halves. Grep-checkable: no `filter: url` on body-text containers, frost `@supports` block present, `prefers-reduced-motion` block present, `prefers-reduced-transparency` block present (near-opaque panel replacement — Apple's own Liquid Glass behavior, atlas §4.3), explicit tight filter regions, no filter on `body`/full-viewport wrappers. Visual (browser QA): no glass over a blank ground, no glass card grid, no decorative wobble on controls, glass-surface text contrast re-checked with the scrim in place.
6. **Range.** The examples directory demonstrates one brief built as two committed material systems (glass treatment vs goo treatment), each with its DESIGN.md law — proving family choice is a real decision, not a default.
7. **Eval complete.** The A/B eval (4 briefs × 2 arms) ran end-to-end, the grid was reviewed by the user, and the verdict is recorded in this spec.

## Architecture (approved: Approach A — overlay skill + computed-optics script)

```
svgf-design/

    materials-map.md            # stance→material map (18 stances), register ceilings, dosage, ban table

    goo.md                      # metaball/organic; delight-moment budget; interaction morphs

    lighting.md                 # specular/diffuse; serving glass edges; badges/seals

    make-glass-map.test.mjs     # node:test suite
  examples/
```

Installed by symlinking `svgf-design/` into `~/.claude/skills/svgf-design` (same pattern as figma-design). The skill is runtime-neutral: no Claude-specific APIs, plain files.

### Trigger vocabulary (the SKILL.md frontmatter description)

Acceptance #1 is decided almost entirely by this one line, so its vocabulary is specified here rather than left to build time. Positive triggers, by family: **glass** (liquid glass, glassy, frosted, refraction, lens, translucent chrome); **goo** (gooey, organic, blob, metaball, liquid/fluid shapes); **grain-print** (grain, paper texture, riso, halftone, duotone, ink, print texture); **lighting** (metallic, specular, embossed, lit material, wet); plus generic material language (premium material, texture-rich, material design system — the physical sense, disambiguated from Google's Material Design). Negative clause, stated in the description: plain UI-design requests without material language stay with figma-design; this skill is invoked *for the material dimension*.

### SKILL.md workflow (four moves layered on figma-design)

1. **Run figma-design as the base.** Classification, ingredient sampling, stance commitment, DESIGN.md authoring, build discipline, QA — all per that skill. If figma-design is not installed, stop and say so.
2. **Material commitment** — written into DESIGN.md immediately after the stance commitment, as law: chosen family (via `materials-map.md`; refined-tech home register when the brief/stance is neutral), the one signature surface, dosage budget (1 signature + ≤2 supporting), ground plan (which of the three sources), fallback-tier plan, register ceiling (product UI = Apple-neat; campaign/brand = Awwwards ceiling).
3. **Build** — recipes from references; for glass, run `make-glass-map.mjs` and paste its output rather than hand-writing filter stacks.
4. **Material QA** — figma-design's QA plus a two-part pass against the DESIGN.md material law. Grep-checkable: signature moment present (`<filter` / `filter: url(` / `backdrop-filter: url(`); frost `@supports (backdrop-filter: blur(1px))` block present; refraction tier NOT gated by `@supports (backdrop-filter: url())`; `prefers-reduced-motion` and `prefers-reduced-transparency` blocks present; explicit tight filter regions on every `<filter>`; no filter on `body` or full-viewport wrappers; no `filter: url` on body-text containers. Visual (browser QA, required for glass): no glass over a blank ground; no glass-card-grid repetition; controls undistorted; text-on-glass contrast verified with the scrim layer, not the blur, providing it. Performance rules inherited from atlas §4.2: prefer CSS shorthand where an equivalent exists; displacement maps rebuilt on resize only.

### The script: `make-glass-map.mjs`

- **Input:** geometry — width/height (or aspect), corner radius, bezel width, refraction strength, shape (`pill` | `squircle` | `rect`).
- **Method:** kube.io engineered optics — per-pixel refraction vector from the bezel profile's surface normal; optically flat center (R=G=128 neutral); fully opaque map (premultiplied-alpha rule).
- **Output:** ready-to-paste block — PNG data-URI inside `feImage` → `feDisplacementMap`, full filter stack with specular edge, plus paired CSS for both tiers (capability-gated refraction + `@supports`-gated frost). Maps are generated at **2× the target pixel size** (banding in a steep bezel gradient becomes visible banding in the refraction).
- **Responsive sizing:** displacement maps do not auto-track element size (atlas §2.1 — a stretched map warps the bezel profile). Rule: signature glass chrome is sized in px (fixed intrinsic size); responsive layouts use per-breakpoint maps at the declared widths; stretch beyond ~20% of the generated width requires regeneration, per the atlas's rebuild-on-resize-only guidance.
- **Dependencies:** node built-ins only (zlib for PNG encoding).
- **Tests (`node:test`):** center neutrality, radial symmetry, strength scaling, PNG validity, filter-block snapshot — plus two contract-level assertions from the atlas's portable subset: the emitted filter block carries `color-interpolation-filters="sRGB"` (without it the 128-neutral drifts in linearRGB, atlas §1.13), and every map pixel has alpha=255 (transparent map pixels displace undefinedly, premultiplied-alpha rule).
- **Risk containment:** this is the project's one real engineering risk → the plan gives it a **spike task with visual browser verification** before any reference or example depends on it. Spike checklist: (a) rendered refraction reads as curved-glass optics (edge bending, stable center) in Chromium; (b) the chosen refraction-tier gating mechanism (no-op overlay or runtime probe) verified in Chromium, Safari, and Firefox — including the atlas's two [uncertain] flags: Firefox's `@supports (backdrop-filter: url())` result and the no-op-overlay behavior; (c) map-stretch behavior observed (how far a fluid container can stretch the map before the bezel visibly warps). Promote-or-discard criterion: if per-pixel computed optics can't be made to read as glass, the discard fallback is the atlas §2.1 **static gradient-ramp map** (two overlapping linear/radial gradients encoding red-horizontal/green-vertical ramps, flat-128 center) — still computed edge-only optics with a stable center, never turbulence wobble (which locked decision 3 and the Approach-B rejection both rule out). A discard triggers a revision of acceptance #4's wording and a note here.

### Eval plan

Four briefs × two arms (figma-design alone vs figma-design + svgf-design), same worker model both arms, browser QA allowed in both arms (glass demands visual verification), side-by-side grid, user judges.

Briefs cover the registers: spatial/media product (home turf — glass), playful consumer (goo), editorial/craft (grain-print), and one deliberately neutral brief (tests the home-register default kicks in). Mechanical assertion set runs alongside human judgment: signature present, tiers present, bans hold, reduced-motion honored.

## Out of scope (this deliverable)

- Squigglevision / animated-displacement wobble and standalone color-grade art directions (duotone as *print* technique is in; duotone as *standalone grade* is v2).
- WebGL, canvas-rendered runtime effects, JS interaction engines (interaction morphs are class toggles between precomputed filter states).
- Component-library richness (shadcn/icon/chart environments — Make's environmental advantage, not a skill concern).
- Changes to figma-design itself beyond what already shipped (its typography Leaks 1 and 3 remain separately deferred).

## Decision Log

- **Approach A (overlay skill + computed-optics script) over B (pure recipe library) and C (full material engine).** B rejected: cannot guarantee the signature moment, and glass quality caps at turbulence-approximated wobble — essentially effects-policy.md again, whose outputs the user already judged as missing the material dimension. C rejected: triple build surface, and it over-mechanizes goo/grain/lighting, which are well-served by recipes plus stance-driven taste; per-family engines would also make v2 families expensive. A puts computation exactly where physics demands it (glass) and taste where taste works (the other families).
- **Invocation-as-earning over keep-the-conservative-gate.** The richer-dial alternative (recipes behind the same earned-effects test) risked reproducing the AI-conservatism the skill exists to escape; a standalone effects-forward designer would duplicate the just-shipped workflow and let two laws drift.
- **Refined-tech home register over stance-neutrality or sampled material family.** The user named the Apple-neat center explicitly; sampling the material family would erase it.
- **Tiered glass over Chromium-only or universal-only.** Chromium-only breaks on Safari — the platform of the aesthetic being chased; universal-only outlaws `backdrop-filter: url()` and with it the flagship effect.
- **Frost-as-supporting-tier over engineered-or-nothing.** Engineered-or-nothing would make the Safari tier self-contradictory and outlaw legitimate quiet frost sheets.
- **Dark-SaaS register (Linear/Vercel) deliberately not a north star** — user kept Apple + kube.io + Awwwards-expressive; the expressive ceiling stays high.
- **One brief → two treatments for examples** (FRAME/MERIDIAN pattern reused) over independent showcase pages: proves material choice is a decision space.

## Surprises & Discoveries

- **Spec review round 1 (2026-07-25) caught the spec mandating a gate the project's own research says is broken:** acceptance #4 originally required an "`@supports`-gated" refraction fallback, but the atlas (§2.8) documents that `@supports (backdrop-filter: url())` validates syntax, not rendering — Firefox would report support and ship broken glass, and the mechanical QA would have passed while locked decision 6 silently failed. Same round: the spike's discard path originally fell back to "best static recipe," which would have landed the flagship exactly where Approach B was rejected (turbulence wobble); replaced with the atlas §2.1 gradient-ramp map, which stays computed optics. Lesson: acceptance criteria that name a mechanism must be cross-checked against the research corpus that studied that mechanism.

- **Task 1 spike (2026-07-25) verdict: PROMOTE, with the architecture corrected after a review finding overturned the first-pass reading of question (a).** Built the kube.io-method prototype (`figma-design-workspace/glass-spike/proto-glass-map.mjs`, transcribed verbatim from the plan) and verified all three spike questions with browser screenshots (Chromium via playwright-cli, WebKit/Firefox via `npx playwright@1.54`).
  - **(a) Optics, Chromium — CORRECTED.** The original recorded verdict ("reads as glass, no sign flip needed," citing `chromium-rim-topleft.png`/`chromium-rim-botright.png`) overclaimed: direct re-inspection of those two screenshots showed the diagonal ground stripes running perfectly straight up to an undistorted boundary, with no ground-pattern content visible inside the panel — no refraction at all. Root cause: `proto-glass-map.mjs`'s original markup nests `.glass-refract` **inside** `.glass`, and `.glass` itself carries `backdrop-filter: blur(14px) saturate(160%)` (the frost tier). Per the atlas's own §2.8 caveat (line 446): "`backdrop-filter` samples the backdrop root: an ancestor with its own filter/opacity/transform can change what 'behind' means" — an element with `backdrop-filter` becomes the backdrop root for its descendants, so the nested `.glass-refract` was displacing the parent's own near-uniform frosted tint, not the sharp ground. Displacing a uniform field is invisible; this is exactly the trap the atlas documents, and the original spike walked into it.
    - **Experiment 1 (isolation, `proto-glass-map-v1-isolated.mjs` → `spike-page-v1-isolated.html`):** rebuilt with NO frost anywhere and `.glass-refract` as the only filter in the page, over a sharp ground. Result: displacement renders clearly. `v1-isolated-topleft-zoom.png` and `v1-isolated-botright-zoom.png` (4×-upscaled, contrast-enhanced crops of the rounded corners) show the diagonal ground stripe visibly curling into a lens shape as it crosses the rim at both corners, while stripes outside the panel stay straight. A pixel-stdev check confirmed the mechanism further: panel-interior R-channel stdev in a text-free strip was 5.4 vs 5.8 for the same-size raw-ground strip — i.e. the flat center passes ground content through essentially unaltered (as expected: map center = neutral 128 = zero displacement), while the bend is concentrated at the bezel as designed.
    - **Experiment 2 (sibling architecture, the production candidate — `proto-glass-map-v2-sibling.mjs` → `spike-page-v2.html`):** parent `.glass` now carries no filter/backdrop-filter/opacity; three siblings (`.glass-frost`, `.glass-refract`, `.glass-tint`) paint in order over the real page. `.glass-refract`'s filter chain was extended to reproduce the whole look itself (it paints over the frost layer in Chromium): `feImage(map) → feDisplacementMap(scale 60) → feGaussianBlur(stdDeviation 3) → feColorMatrix(saturate 1.6)`. First draft had a bug — `.glass` carried an unconditional 92%-opaque background meant only as the no-backdrop-filter fallback, which sat under the children and swamped what they sampled; fixed by gating it to `@supports not (backdrop-filter: blur(1px))`. After the fix, `v2-chromium-full.png` shows a properly frosted pill (soft blurred/saturated interior matching the ground's color story, legible "Liquid glass" label). Raw-screenshot rim bending is subtle to the eye — expected, since `blur(3)` on top of the displacement softens the sharp edge that experiment 1 showed uncomposited — so two independent checks were used to confirm it's real, not a second null result: (1) grayscale-equalized crops of both rounded corners (`v2-chromium-topleft-eq.png`, `v2-chromium-botright-eq.png`) show a clear swirl/warp in the pattern hugging each rim, versus straight diagonal stripes immediately outside the panel; (2) a quantitative control — a second render with `feDisplacementMap` scale forced to 0 (`spike-page-v2-control.html`, `v2-control-full.png`), diffed pixel-by-pixel against the scale-60 version (`v2-chromium-full.png`) — shows per-column max RGB diff spiking to 37–41 (of a 255 per-channel scale, summed across 3 channels) in exactly the bezel band (local x 4–28 and 328–352, either side of the 360px-wide, 16px-bezel pill) and staying flat at 2–3 everywhere in the flat center zone (x 40–320). Displacement is real, spatially exact to the bezel, and invisible at the optical center — precisely the intended behavior.
    - **Verdict: refraction is real but requires the sibling architecture, not the original nested-overlay one.** No sign-flip was needed once refraction was actually visible (Experiment 1's lens-not-pinch bend confirms the original sign convention). The corrected mechanism Task 2 must ship: sibling layering (frost / refract / tint as separate children of an unfiltered `.glass` parent, `@supports not` gating the opaque fallback background) plus the full filter chain on the refract layer (displace → blur(3) → saturate(1.6)), because in Chromium that layer paints over, not under, the frost.
  - **(b) Gating, all three engines — description updated for the sibling architecture.** The **layered no-op overlay** mechanism still wins, but the layering requirement is now sibling, not nested, and the Chromium filter chain must include the frost's blur+saturate itself (see (a)). WebKit (`webkit.png`/`webkit-closeup.png`, and re-verified for the v2 sibling markup as `webkit-v2.png`) and Firefox 140.0.2 (`firefox.png`/`firefox-closeup.png`, re-verified as `firefox-v2.png`) both render the frost tier correctly — blurred, saturated, legible — with `.glass-refract` (`backdrop-filter: url(#glass-refract-full)`) present and doing nothing harmful in either engine, confirming the no-op-overlay contract survives the architecture change. The atlas's two `[uncertain]` flags remain resolved as originally recorded: **Firefox's `CSS.supports('backdrop-filter', 'url(#glass-refract)')` returns `true`**, and **WebKit also returns `true`** for the same probe — both are false positives exactly as atlas §2.8 predicted (`CSS.supports` validates syntax, not rendering), which is precisely why locked decision 6's ban on `@supports`-gating the refraction tier is correct and necessary. Net: the sibling overlay is the mechanism Task 2 should ship; the UA-sniffing probe fallback in the brief was still not needed.
  - **(c) Stretch tolerance — unaffected by the architecture correction, one-line note only.** Measured at 400px (11%), 430px (19%), 470px (30%), and 80vw (≈1024px, ≈2.8×) against the 360px-native map, with a text-rich ground for contrast. **No visible bezel warp through 470px/30%** — left and right corners stayed clean, symmetric, and equally well-frosted at every intermediate width (`pill-360/400/430/470.png`, `470-leftedge.png`/`470-rightedge.png`). Visible, **asymmetric** degradation first appeared only at the extreme 80vw/2.8× point: the left edge (still inside the map's native 0–360 local-px span) stayed cleanly frosted, while the right edge (entirely beyond the map's native width, sampling the map's undefined/transparent region) showed visibly weaker frosting — background text reads more sharply, less obscured, right up to the rim (`80vw-leftedge.png` vs `80vw-rightedge.png`). This matches the predicted mechanism: `feImage` only covers local x 0–360, so pixels beyond that get a constant, un-falloff'd displacement rather than a proper rim lens. **Verdict: the spec's ~20% working bound is confirmed safe and conservative** — measured tolerance is at least 30% before any visible artifact. Note: this measurement was taken against the original nested-overlay markup, but the stretch/tiling math (map local-px span vs. element width) is identical under the sibling architecture — the finding transfers unchanged.
  - **Promote/discard: PROMOTE (verdict unchanged, mechanism corrected).** All three questions resolved favorably once the sibling architecture replaced the nested one; Task 2 hardens the corrected markup and filter chain (`proto-glass-map-v2-sibling.mjs`'s approach, not the original nested `proto-glass-map.mjs` one) into `scripts/make-glass-map.mjs`, rather than falling back to the atlas §2.1 gradient-ramp map. Acceptance #4's wording stands unchanged (no discard triggered).
