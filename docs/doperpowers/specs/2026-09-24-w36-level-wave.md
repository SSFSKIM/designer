## Purpose

inside the edge, where no rim, lens or scatter acts — misses Apple's LEVEL by more than the edge
line it was looking for: **+49 codes on light black, +28 on dark black**, +3…+8 on light mid-greys,
−1…−7 on dark mid-greys, and **up to 111 (active) and 144 (inactive) codes on saturated channels**.
The memo found three mechanisms, not one wrong curve:

1. **Black is a fallback, not a curve point.** The tone solve's authority is
   `smoothstep(anchor/2, anchor, x)` with the first knot's abscissa 0.004 (`wgsl/optics.ts` ~1168;
   mirrored on the CSS tier at `platform-web/src/optics.ts` ~616). At a pure-black backdrop it is

   floored at 0 (the dark receded document, whose unexplained +0.0475 floor in §5.154 §6 is, by
   arithmetic, probably this same fallback). Restoring authority alone would render −4 / −20 / +21 /
   −20 codes against native (light active / light inactive / dark active / dark inactive): the knot
   itself must be fitted on uniform black.
2. **The curve's middle sits where `photo` put it.** The shipped response reproduces all 48
   non-black web medians on the bed within 0.5 code — the web body's luma IS the curve — and its
   third knot (0.425, fitted on `photo`, a chromatic structured backdrop, W29 G3) sits +0.05 linear
   above Apple's uniform-grey curve at the same input. Structure alone barely moves Apple's level
   (the neutral checkerboard reads within 1–3 codes of grey-128), so the excess is either photo's
   chroma coupling into luma or a knot in the wrong place; the greys decide.

   backdrop's linear chromaticity whole on the dark scheme (implied retention 0.95–1.03) and at

   retention is what rotates hue. The receded pose's abscissa (the silhouette's per-channel
